## Method

- **Regulations:** eCFR point-in-time text current as of October 29, 2026, read through the eCFR versioner API. Last amendment dates at that point: Title 2 on August 17, 2026; Title 23 on September 23, 2026; Title 28 on October 27, 2026; Titles 40 and 49 on October 28, 2026. Each row links the current eCFR section.
- **Statutes:** the 2024 U.S. Code edition on GovInfo. The Office of the Law Revision Counsel site (uscode.house.gov) runs in a temporary format and returned "page not found" for section requests; it reports currency through Pub. L. 119-136 (October 22, 2026). Amendments enacted after the 2024 edition were not checked section by section.
- **Agency documents:** FTA, FHWA and USDOT pages refuse scripted requests (HTTP 403), so they were read in a browser on November 1, 2026: the 2026 NTD Full Reporting Policy Manual, FTA Circular 4702.1B, FTA's triennial and state management review pages, the FY25 SS4A General Terms and Conditions, USDOT's Comprehensive Safety Action Plans page and FHWA Notice N 4510.916. Federal Register documents came from the Federal Register API.
- **California:** Government Code sections from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, read in a browser, and the Caltrans 2017 Regional Planning Handbook.
- **Status rule:** a row is `verified` only when its cadence, threshold and approver were read in the cited primary text. Agency guidance and grant terms count as primary for their own requirements. Search results and other agencies' documents never verify a row. Anything unread stays `[VERIFY]` with what was checked.
- **OpenPlan home:** read from code on main at c2b4ad2: `openplan/src/lib/programs/catalog.ts` (program types), `openplan/src/lib/my-work/types.ts` (My Work sources), `openplan/src/lib/land-use-plans/registry.ts` (California general plan descriptor), `openplan/src/lib/title-vi/` and the capability matrix. **Served:** a record holds the deliverable and My Work reads its date. **Partial:** a module holds part of the deliverable but not the cadence or trigger. **No home:** nothing holds it.

## Federal surface transportation authorization is extended through December 11, 2026

FHWA Notice N 4510.916 (October 1, 2026) states that the Surface Transportation Extension Act of 2026, Division C of Pub. L. 119-103, "extends the surface transportation programs, including the Federal-aid highway program, under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) (Pub. L. 117-58) and Title 23, U.S.C., through December 11, 2026." Section 3101(a) continues the covered requirements and authorities through that date; the covered laws include IIJA Divisions A, B and C. GovInfo dates Pub. L. 119-103, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, September 2, 2026. GovInfo lists no later surface transportation law through Pub. L. 119-136 (October 22, 2026). Authorization after December 11, 2026 is unknown. The cadences cited here sit in codified regulation, codified statute or agency documents. This note did not determine how a lapse would affect them. It would affect the funding levels a TIP or STIP financial plan relies on.

## What this changes for TIP and STIP scoping

1. **The federal TIP is not the Programs "STIP" type.** That type is California's CTC STIP under Gov. Code 14529. A federal TIP covers at least 4 years, is updated at least every 4 years, and is approved by the MPO and the Governor, then by FHWA and FTA through the STIP. The State includes each approved TIP in the STIP without change (23 CFR 450.218(b)). A TIP lane needs its own type and its own approval chain.
2. **Four duties ride on the TIP clock.** Self-certification at each TIP submittal (23 CFR 450.336(a)); conformity of the TIP at least every 4 years in nonattainment and maintenance areas (40 CFR 93.104(c)(3)); the TIP's description of its effect toward the MTP performance targets (23 CFR 450.326(d)); and the annual listing of obligated projects within 90 days after the program year, which reports against TIP amounts (23 CFR 450.334(b)). TAM plan updates "should coincide" with the TIP or STIP cycle (49 CFR 625.29(c)). Scoping the TIP alone would leave these orphaned.
3. **Targets come due soon.** States filed 2026 Baseline Performance Period Reports by October 1, 2026. MPOs must set PM2 and PM3 targets within 180 days after their State does: March 30, 2027 if the State set targets on October 1, 2026, earlier if it set them sooner. PM1 targets recur each year, 180 days after the August 31 HSIP report. A TIP job that shows the target-effect section needs these target records first.
4. **The financial plan needs an authorization assumption.** A TIP financial plan uses revenues "reasonably expected to be made available" in year-of-expenditure dollars (23 CFR 450.326(j)). Federal authorization after December 11, 2026 is unknown, so the TIP record should store the revenue assumption and its source date per TIP, not a fixed IIJA amount.
5. **MPO Title VI investment analyses are uncertain.** Circular 4702.1B Chapter VI asks MPOs for investment analyses that the June 11, 2026 DOT rule calls disparate-impact-related. A TIP lane should not build a required Title VI analysis step until FTA states its current requirement.

## Other findings for the lead

- **The ADA web rule reaches OpenPlan's public pages.** 28 CFR 35.200(a) covers web content a public entity provides "directly or through contractual, licensing, or other arrangements." Engagement pages and published plans an agency serves through OpenPlan are that agency's content. Large entities must meet WCAG 2.1 AA by April 26, 2027; small entities and special districts by April 26, 2028. This supports the direction review's "portal accessibility proof" item with a date.
- **The Title VI service-equity calculation keeps its local-policy basis but may lose its federal one.** `openplan/src/lib/title-vi/policy.ts` treats disparate-impact thresholds as adopted agency policy with no defaults, which remains sound. Its framing as an FTA requirement needs review after the June 11, 2026 rule. No code changed here.
- **California circulation element revisions now carry Safe System content.** Since January 1, 2025, a substantive circulation element revision must include Safe System policies and bicycle, pedestrian and traffic calming plans for urbanized areas (Gov. Code 65302(b)(2)(B)). This ties Land Use Plans to Safety.
- **Q6 falsifier, partly tested.** Reviewer A's falsifier was that drafts for three organization types in five states show most duties are agency-specific. In the federal set every cadence comes from statute, regulation, an FTA circular or policy manual, or grant terms; none is agency-specific. Six of 48 rows are unverified, and two of those (FHWA subrecipient Title VI, state management review) are set by States or FTA regions. Only California's state duties were drafted, so the five-state condition is untested.
- **Workspaces carry no organization type.** The registry needs to know whether a workspace is an MPO, an RTPA, a transit agency or a city. `openplan/src/lib/jurisdiction-readiness/` uses broader organization ids (`regional-and-metropolitan-organizations`, `local-and-county-government`); nothing records TMA status, air quality designation, FTA reporter type or population. Those are the thresholds most rows depend on.
