# OpenPlan pull requests #100 to #135: two-month retrospective

Reviewed 2026-10-11 against `main` at 05dd3e8da. This was a static review only: no tests, builds or servers ran.

## Bottom line

- **All 36 merged, none closed.** Every head commit is on `main`, so no branch work was lost.
- **21 of 36 are user-visible.** 16 add a planner capability and 5 fix something a planner sees. The other 15 are internal: custody, recovery, evidence, integration, release and build work.
- **Most added lines are evidence, not code.** Application source (excluding tests) is 9 percent of lines added in September and about 8 percent in October. Committed evidence (logs, JSON receipts, screenshots, probe scripts under `docs/reviews/`) is 48 percent in September and about 80 percent in October.
- **No human reviewed any of the 36.**
  - The Codex bot reviewed 21, failed on 1, hit its usage limit on 12 and was never triggered on 2.
  - 12 merged within an hour of opening. The median from open to merge was 101 minutes.
- **18 of 28 Codex findings are still open on `main` (9 P1, 9 P2).**
  - Of the other 10: 5 were fixed in code before merge, 3 were answered with added browser evidence, 1 was likely fixed later on `main`, and 1 became moot.
  - Three of the open findings are money defects in OWP and contract billing.
  - None of the 18 is recorded in `docs/ops/KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
- **Docs kept up in September but not in October.**
  - In September, five pull requests updated the roadmap, changelog, known issues and ledger together.
  - In October, no pull request touched known issues and only one touched the ledger.
  - Two land-use claims in the roadmap and known issues are now stale.

## Method and caveats

- **Inputs were reused, not refetched.** Metadata, bodies, Codex comments and commit lists came from the earlier attempt's files in this folder.
- **Sizes are each pull request's own change.** GitHub's sizes count stacked predecessors: #130 shows +130,985 lines, but its own change is +12,082. The own delta is the diff from the nearest ancestor, either `main` or another pull request's head (`scripts/delta3.py`).
  - #100, #101 and #111 were fast-forwarded, so they are measured from their first commit's parent.
  - #113 is measured against `main` minus #112.
- **Line categories:**
  - `src` is non-test code under `openplan/src`.
  - `test` is test code and fixtures.
  - `mig` is SQL migrations.
  - `evid` is anything under `docs/reviews/` or an `evidence/` folder.
- **"Open on `main`" means the flagged code is unchanged** in the latest definition on `main`. I did not run the scenarios, so a guard I did not trace may block some of them.
- **These 36 pull requests are a minority of the period's work.**
  - From September 5 to October 8, `main` received 441 direct commits and 42 merges.
  - No pull requests were opened between September 9 (#111) and October 2 (#112). Releases v0.48 through v0.66 shipped in that gap with no pull request and no bot review.

## Synthesis

### Themes

1. **September built agency administration.** Six of 12 pull requests added a capability: OWP review, reporting and reimbursement, plus contract reconciliation and closeout. Docs moved with the code. Codex found real financial-logic defects, and most were not fixed.
2. **October built custody.** 10 of 24 pull requests added a capability, and much of that is plumbing for retry receipts, frozen editions and verified downloads.
   - Engagement got a five-step permission pipeline for machine comment summaries, with no demonstrated useful output.
   - Land use got 11 pull requests in one day, most binding reports and downloads to frozen editions.
3. **Two pieces are finished planner work, with the gaps listed above:** the BCA workbench (#118) and the September OWP and contract cycle (#103 to #110).

### User-visible versus internal

- **By count:** 21 user-visible (16 capabilities, 5 fixes) and 15 internal.
- **By lines added, September:** application source 9 percent (5,869 of 62,420), evidence 48 percent, tests 13 percent.
- **By lines added, October:** application source about 8 percent (about 17,700 of 229,000), evidence about 80 percent, tests 10 percent.
- **Evidence per source line:** the median October pull request added about 8 evidence lines per source line. #128 to #130 together added about 49,700 evidence lines for 295 source lines.

### Recurring problems

- **Quality:**
  - Review findings that need a logic change stay open.
  - Geography rules leak into core code.
  - One-line JSX defeats review.
  - UI copy states engineering limits instead of telling a planner what to do.
- **Documentation:**
  - Known issues were abandoned after September 30.
  - The ledger skipped land use and the BCA.
  - The roadmap is used as an evidence index.
  - The M1 claims are stale.
  - Pull request bodies run 500 to 1,000 words of hashes and test counts, and no person read them.

### Observations for roadmap replanning

1. **Fix the open findings before adding features.** Start with the three money defects: duplicate hours (#104), stranded billing after a void (#106), and approval that skips PM review (#107). Log all 18 in `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` first. Each is a small SQL or UI change.
2. **Cap the evidence and move raw logs to CI artifacts.** Evidence is 80 percent of what October added, and producing it is where the agent hours went. Now that the subscription funding has ended, keep one short verification note per pull request in the repository.
3. **Point engagement back at CORE-ENG-01 and 02:** the public map and the planner dashboard. Hold further synthesis custody work until one nonempty run produces a summary a planner would use.
4. **Replan M1 around its own "done" cases.** None of these was exercised by #123 to #133:
   - a California plan in an Oregon workspace, and the reverse;
   - a missing home jurisdiction;
   - a multistate MPO;
   - a tribal authority.

   Also remove the US and TIGERweb literals from core.
5. **Split the roadmap.** Make it a short queue page a planner can read in two minutes, plus a separate evidence index. Correct the stale M1 and KL-2026-09-04-002 claims while doing it.
