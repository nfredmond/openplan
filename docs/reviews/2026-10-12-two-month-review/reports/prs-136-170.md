## Synthesis

### 1. What the range actually is

Thirty-five PRs, two days, one author, zero human reviews, every merge a self-merge. The bot reviewer was over its usage cap for 20 of the 35, including every PR from #158 to #170, which is where 70 percent of the source lines landed. Three PRs merged within an hour of opening; the median for the Oct 8 batch was about 100 minutes, which is the time CI takes, not the time review takes. The four final PRs sat 30 hours, but the hold was for CI and a self-discovered defect, not for a reader.

The 35 numbers describe about 20 units of work. Eight PRs are stacked re-submissions that carry every ancestor's files again (#138, #143, #147, #148, #155, #168, #169, #170), and eight were closed because a successor already contained them. The stacking inflates file counts (#169 and #170 report 100 files each, 60 of them identical prototype scripts) and makes `gh pr diff` useless for seeing what one PR changed.

### 2. Planner-visible versus internal

By PR count among the 27 merged: 13 touch something a planner can see, 14 do not. By source lines it is lopsided the other way: #136 to #148 and #155 together add about 650 lines of application source; #157 to #170 add about 5,200 lines of worker, migration and library code that no planner can reach yet. The three largest PRs by evidence (#169, #170, #138) add 15,900 lines under `docs/reviews/` against 2,000 of source.

What a planner got in two days: source labels and an "Approved" label on engagement exports; a queue button and a "Maximum task bytes" field for synthesis; three small evidence corrections on project and model cards; an award that no longer vanishes from My Work when closed, and a reopen form that asks for a status; a calendar date that stops shifting a day; a published model card that refuses to show unverified numbers. All of these are corrections to things that already existed. None is a new capability against M2d, M9, M11, M12 or M13.

What a planner also got, without asking: on any installation that applies migration 18, every existing AequilibraE or ActivitySim run is now labelled "Reconciliation required," loses Relaunch and its progress bar, and refuses database writes. No reconciliation tool shipped (#169 body: "operator reconciliation ... remain incomplete"). Local artifact downloads stop working on non-Linux hosts (#170). Workspaces holding retained model commands cannot be deleted (#170). The model creator now blocks without a project or scenario link (#169). None of the four appears in CHANGELOG as a planner-facing consequence; the CHANGELOG entries for #161 to #170 are all written as migration-application notices for operators.

### 3. Recurring quality problems

- Correctness findings left open. Four of the twelve bot findings were fixed. The P1 on #144 (closed awards reported "overdue" in the digest) is a wrong statement in an email planners receive, and it is still on main. The rules-version finding on #148 means two assessments with different pass/fail semantics share `rules_version: 4`.
- Copy-and-paste across the two workers. `_confirmed_state_patch` (#157) is duplicated verbatim in `aequilibrae_worker/main.py` and `activitysim_worker/supabase_poll.py`, with a comment admitting it is a mirror. Every subsequent receipt helper followed the same pattern.
- Tests that count instead of name. The sweep tests in #145/#147/#148 changed six hardcoded totals (9 to 10) to pass. The column-ledger test gained 24 exemptions for columns nothing reads (#161) plus more in #164, #165, #166, #169. That ledger exists to ask "why does nothing read this"; it is now a list of permitted silences.
- Merging through a recorded hold. #168's own body says "do not merge it separately" and documents a KPI-deleting defect; #161, #162 and #164 each record a failed full-QA run and then merge on a later partial run. The hold discipline in COORDINATION.md was not applied to these PR bodies.
- UI text volume and engineering vocabulary. "Maximum task bytes" with four sentences of explanation (#139); "Queue custody does not prove that a call ran" (#139); "Do not restart the computation until its database records, worker journal and output files have been reconciled" (#169). The owner's Oct 10 instruction was less text and no build notes; this range went the other way. Every hedging sentence in a PR body migrated into UI copy or CHANGELOG.
- Executable code under `docs/`. #159, #160 and the prototype directory hold 2,000-plus lines of SQL and Python runners that duplicate migration 14. Mutation logs (#138: 50-plus `sql-*.log` files) are checked in as review records. Evidence volume is 5.3 times source volume.
- Platform assumptions. Linux-only artifact reads (#170), Node 24 and Linux-only unit generation (#150), `/proc/self/fd` traversal. No geography literals in application source, which is good; the Butte/Merced references stay in evidence files.
- No rendered acceptance for the biggest UI change. #169/#170 shipped the recovery notice, relaunch removal and model-creator gate with "snapshots fail even on the small health-response page; no ... visual acceptance is claimed."

### 4. Recurring documentation gaps

- KNOWN_ISSUES.md was not touched by any of the 35 PRs. The #168 KPI-deletion defect, the "all historical runs read-only with no reconciliation path" state, the non-Linux download refusal, the workspace-deletion refusal, and the open Codex P1 on #144 are all absent from it.
- ROADMAP edits are evidence links, not status. Every ROADMAP change in the range appends a dated bullet with a link and a "does not complete" hedge. No milestone moved, and the M3 work from #157 to #170 was filed under S1 bullets rather than M3, which is the milestone it serves.
- The ledger was updated three times (#137/#138, #148/#149, #153/#155), each as a 12 to 29 line paragraph of hedges. CORE-MODEL-01 and CORE-HOST-01, which the worker work most affects, were not touched.
- docs/README "Current authorities" is one paragraph that grew to about 1,500 words across the range, with 20-plus inline links. It is not readable as a table of contents.
- CHANGELOG entries from #161 onward read "Migration N adds private receipts. Apply it before..." They tell an operator what to run and tell a planner nothing. The v0.68.0 front-matter bump (#143) preceded the tag by a day.
- PR bodies are the real changelog. The only complete statement of what #169 changes for a user is in its PR body, not in any tracked doc.

### 5. Observations for roadmap replanning

1. Migration 18 shipped a fence with no gate. Every installation that upgrades past v0.68.0 will see all historical model runs marked "Reconciliation required" with relaunch removed and writes refused, and there is no reconciliation tool. Before any further model-recovery work, decide whether to ship a reconciliation command, a bulk "accept as historical" action, or a reversal. This is the single most planner-visible consequence of the range and it is documented only in a PR body.

2. Two days produced about 650 lines of planner-reachable source and 5,200 lines of unreachable custody code, plus 34,000 lines of evidence. At the post-funding hourly budget, that ratio does not move M2d, M9, M11, M12 or M13. The replanning should set an explicit cap on evidence-per-source or move mutation logs out of the repository, and should route the next increment to a milestone with a planner outcome.

3. Review is absent, and the one reviewer that existed was mostly out of credit. Twelve findings, four fixed, one open P1 in a planner-facing email. Either fund the bot for the PRs that touch workers and migrations, or stop merging those within the same session they were written. A written rule that a PR body containing "hold" or "do not merge" cannot be merged by the author would have caught #168, #161, #162 and #164.

4. Stacked PRs should stop. Eight of 35 were pure re-submissions and eight more were closed as already-merged. The cost is that per-PR diffs, file counts and CHANGELOG hunks are unreadable, and that doc edits (ROADMAP, ledger) appear in three PRs each. One PR per unit of work, rebased on main, would halve the count and make the record usable.

5. Documentation is in the wrong places. KNOWN_ISSUES was untouched while five new known issues appeared; ROADMAP accumulated dated bullets instead of status; CHANGELOG became an operator migration log; docs/README became a run-on paragraph; and UI copy absorbed the hedging language from PR bodies. The replan should name one place for each of: what changed for planners (CHANGELOG), what is broken (KNOWN_ISSUES), what operators must do (RUNBOOK or an upgrade guide), and what evidence exists (docs/reviews index), and should apply the Oct 10 "less text" directive to the three new panels (#139 queue, #139 task bytes, #169 recovery notice).
