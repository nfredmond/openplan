## Texas differences that matter

The Texas family separates policy from operating procedures. Its stated scope excludes public transportation, aviation and concessionaire projects. A state selector therefore cannot imply that this manual covers every transportation job. Existing planning and modal workflows must retain their own authorities. [Policy introduction, printed 1-2–1-6](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/txdotoms/tpd/lgp/lgp.pdf).

An AFA allocates project participation between TxDOT and the local government; it is explicitly not the procurement contract with a consultant or contractor. It assigns work, resources and funding. Who performs work can differ from who pays. Federal project authorization remains a distinct prerequisite. Model the funding agreement and delivery contracts as linked records with separate parties and approvals. [Chapter 2, AFA overview](https://www.txdot.gov/manuals/tpd/lgp/project_initiation/advance_funding_agreements-i1007092/overview-i1007097.html).

Texas distinguishes the full-time public Responsible Person in Charge, a qualified person who may be a consultant, and the project manager. Qualification does not confer public authority. Fixed-price versus specified-percentage participation also changes who bears overruns; written amendments change obligations. These are important counterexamples to one project-owner role and a universal local-match percentage. [Chapter 2 §4](https://www.txdot.gov/manuals/tpd/lgp/project_initiation/advance_funding_agreements-i1007092.html). Current training guidance requires the qualified person to complete the LGPP course at least every three years. [Training and qualification](https://www.txdot.gov/business/resources/lgp/training-and-qualification.html).

The December 2025 guidance separates agency risk assessment, approval to manage particular project elements and later project reevaluation. Agency risk influences reassessment frequency. Consultant experience cannot replace the contracting agency's own experience in special approval. District/division recommendations proceed to the specified executive approval; staff turnover or performance can change oversight on active projects. Store agency assessment and project delegation independently, including actual authority and expiration/reassessment triggers. [Risk guidance, printed 5–11](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/tpd/lgp/local-government-risk-assessments-guidance.pdf).

Cash can flow both ways. The guide describes reimbursement requests with status/schedule, monthly billing expectations, local advances to TxDOT, and final reconciliation/refund or additional collection according to the AFA. MPO non-construction projects can involve TPP handling final cost statements. This requires a funding ledger beyond consultant invoices or a single reimbursable balance. [Guide §2.6, printed 2-36–2-38](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/tpd/lgp/guide.pdf).

For covered professional services, Texas requires its qualifications-based process or an alternate process approved in writing, plus Consultant Concurrence before contracting. The chapter distinguishes statutory professional services and separately addresses other goods/services. Do not classify all planning work solely by an RFP/RFQ label or copy Caltrans approval names. [Chapter 3 §2](https://www.txdot.gov/manuals/tpd/lgp/nonconstruction_projects/professional_services-i1002637.html).

## Florida differences that matter

FDOT describes full agency certification lasting three years and project-specific certification renewed for a new project. Certification examines staff, procurement, civil rights, construction administration and materials capability. A training credential alone cannot establish agency eligibility. [LAP certification](https://www.fdot.gov/programmanagement/LAP/BecomingCertified.shtm).

The selected manual sections add specific boundaries: Chapter 7.3.1 retains NEPA class-of-action, environmental and right-of-way certification decisions with FDOT. Chapter 6.5 requires an invoice/status-report packet and underlying evidence, generally at least quarterly. Proof includes agency-to-prime and prime-to-sub payment, except the approved alternative-payment procedure. Missing or incorrect documentation returns for correction. Chapter 5 places monitoring and financial agreement responsibilities with the grant manager. These are separate decisions and records, not one completed project checklist. [Consolidated manual, printed 5-4–5-7, 6-9–6-11, 7-9–7-12](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/fpo/lp/manual/local-programs-manual-2025.pdf?sfvrsn=92e7c249_1).

Chapter 14 bars proceeding beyond negotiation before the required federal authorization and FDOT NTP; contract execution follows authorization and concurrence. It requires department-prequalified consultants for classifications A/B/C and allows an agency qualification process for D. The actual current prequalification letter matters, not merely appearing on a list. Work-type qualifications and conflict certifications must follow the solicitation. This is a different configured approval route from California's. The chapter limits its federal professional-services scope; locally funded work still has applicable state law. [Chapter 14.1–14.3, printed 14-3–14-5](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/fpo/lp/manual/local-programs-manual-2025---chapter-14.pdf?sfvrsn=4ec2bf0d_1).

Two current updates are particularly useful acceptance cases:

- Bulletin 26-02 applies to construction-phase LAP agreements executed on or after July 1, 2026. It requires locally held construction contingency of the greater of 0.5% of the original contract amount or $10,000, reflected in funding schedules. Depletion evidence precedes a shortfall-funding request. Treat this as a dated Florida requirement, not a nationwide estimate default. [June 22, 2026 bulletin, pp.1–3](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/fpo/bulletins/fpo/2026/forecasting-and-performance-bulletin-26-02.pdf?sfvrsn=f0c2c2a5_1).
- Bulletin 25-01 increases continuing-service ceilings but explicitly excludes existing contracts; open solicitations may change by addendum. Preserving the original advertisement and contract limits is essential. A new global default must not enlarge an old award. [November 18, 2025 bulletin, pp.1–2](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/fpo/bulletins/fpo/2025/forecasting-and-performance-bulletin-25-01.pdf?sfvrsn=60d4cd0d_1).

Bulletin 26-01 adds DRIVE-D/C baseline, independent review and performance procedures, with a design-advertisement trigger. Its instructions describe district-managed teams. Its exact application to particular locally administered arrangements needs reconciliation before creating mandatory LAP tasks. Listing it beside LAP bulletins is insufficient proof. [April 24, 2026 bulletin](https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/fpo/bulletins/fpo/2026/forecasting-and-performance-bulletin-26-01.pdf?sfvrsn=82f1ae85_7).

## Proposed roadmap work and verification

These recommendations extend the California [Chapters 9–12 findings](LAPM_CHAPTERS_09_12_REVIEW.md). This reconnaissance did not reread OpenPlan implementation or establish new code defects.

| Existing home | Concrete addition | Evidence required before acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| M1/M4 | Versioned authority, agency capability, phase delegation and staff credentials | Expired qualification, consultant replacing public authority, wrong district and staff departure produce correct unresolved/review states. |
| M10/M11/M13 | Separate funding agreement, delivery contract, local advance, reimbursement, contingency and final reconciliation | One Texas AFA and one Florida LAP case reconcile to source documents without confusing payment direction, eligibility or retained local cash. |
| M14 | State-specific procurement approvals, qualification evidence and immutable advertised limits | Florida old-contract/new-solicitation cases retain different limits; missing concurrence or prequalification evidence prevents false readiness. |
| M2/M8/M9 | Recurring oversight, civil-rights program evidence and external submission receipts | Agency certification cannot close project-specific environmental approvals; saving a packet cannot mark GAP/TxDOT receipt complete. |

Review Florida fully first because its current updates and agency certification offer strong tests of the shared design. Then read Texas policy and companion guide together, including current special-approval forms. Both reviews need page ledgers, update reconciliation and representative practitioner cases. Subsequent states should challenge the same mechanisms, not receive copied California labels.

Full reviews must refresh underlying statutes, federal transition rules, DBE implementation, specifications, discretionary-award conditions and external-system requirements. Neither state's manual alone covers all planner work. No new module per state is proposed, and no portal integration, legal certification or live submission is claimed.
