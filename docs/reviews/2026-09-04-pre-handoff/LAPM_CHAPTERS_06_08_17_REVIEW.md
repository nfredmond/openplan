## Chapter 6. Environmental procedures

Source: [official chapter 6](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch06.pdf), PDF pages 1–76, printed contents i–ii and 1–74. All read. Figures 6-1 through 6-4, PDF pages 38, 45, 56, 66 and 67, visually read.

The chapter contains an actual multi-organization review process. The local public agency prepares work; Caltrans staff with specified responsibilities review and issue particular determinations; FHWA retains named functions. A project workspace administrator is not automatically any of these authorities. Chapter 6 describes off-State Highway System local assistance separately from locally sponsored on-system projects, which start with PEAR/PDPM. It also names exceptions to assigned federal responsibilities and FHWA government-to-government tribal consultation. Do not select the authority only from the workspace's home geography or let a consultant impersonate a signatory. See §6.1, printed pages 1, 5–13.

The substantive workflow families are:

- **Scoped project and applicability record.** Preserve consistent project description, footprint, alternatives, logical termini, purpose/need and programmed identity. Record NEPA involvement and CEQA review separately, even when a joint document is produced. The NEPA/CEQA classes need not match. Program/project scope changes can reopen programming, conformity and environmental work. See §6.1–6.3, printed pages 13–30.
- **Screening and study authorization.** PES and non-infrastructure PES(NI) use different applicability and review paths. A justified no, an unresolved answer, a required study and a signed determination are distinct. Required studies follow the signed screening and applicable APE approval. Non-infrastructure funding does not erase environmental review. See §6.4–6.7, printed pages 30–39.
- **Document production and repeated quality review.** CE without studies, CE with studies, routine EA, complex EA and EIS have different packages, reviewers and branching conditions. Retain specialist qualifications, original source studies, checklist-to-page references, written deficiencies, response keys, tracked revisions, updated review certifications and independent legal review where applicable. A failed package returns to a specific revision step; it does not restart the entire project. Caltrans legal comments can have restricted recipients and a separate shareable summary. See §6.6–6.10, printed pages 33–73; legal-comment restriction at printed page 66.
- **Consultations, permits and commitments.** Resource-agency consultation, APE/Section 106, Section 4(f), protected species, air-quality conformity, permits and conditions are records with responsible authorities. A saved model output is not the agency's conformity determination. Trace every applicable avoidance, minimization and mitigation commitment into PS&E, the contract, field implementation and ongoing monitoring. See §6.2–6.3 and the final steps of each document pathway.
- **Reevaluation and post-approval custody.** Record the next major federal approval, project change and relevant time trigger; preserve whether an earlier document remains valid, needs updating or needs a new/supplemental process. Track seasonal survey windows and external review targets separately from legal deadlines. Retain environmental records for the applicable period after final reimbursement and longer where mitigation/monitoring obligations require it. See §6.3, printed pages 27–30.

OB26-01 R1 is effective immediately as stated in that February bulletin. It removes specified analysis/terminology from the NEPA portion and expressly retains climate/GHG analysis under CEQA; it also directs agencies to continue Chapter 8 procedures during revision. This demands section-specific requirements and source dates, not a global search-and-delete operation. [Official OB26-01 R1](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/ob/2026/ob26-01r1.pdf).

Current OpenPlan has environmental work-plan templates, project deliverables/milestones, document and evidence packages, plan documents, CEQA screening and stage-gate foundations. Root inspected the complete California template and the named generic deliverable/milestone definitions in `openplan/src/lib/work-plans/templates/ca_environmental_review_v0.1.json` and `environmental_review_v0.1.json`. Their schedule offsets explicitly are placeholders, and the California template explicitly stops at certification/adoption. This honesty is useful; applying a template does not establish a maintained LAPM environmental case. Sampled API/library/migration searches did not establish structured PES, FONSI, reevaluation or mitigation-commitment lifecycle records. This is bounded absence evidence, not an exhaustive search of all possible metadata.

Extend M8/M10b, M2 document review and M4 authority/privacy. Done requires one real supported case in each meaningful class, including non-infrastructure and a changed approved project, with correct external signatory evidence and surviving commitments. Verify premature final-design/advertisement refusal where applicable, changed footprint, missing consultation, unresolved screening, expired permit, wrong actor, unavailable study and legal-summary access. Do not build a separate NEPA module for each class or automate professional findings.

## Chapter 7. Field review

Source: [official chapter 7](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch07.pdf), all 9 PDF pages, printed contents i–ii and 1–7. Figure 7-1 visually read.

Field review is early project definition and interagency agreement, not merely a site photograph or inspection checkbox. The record captures project manager, interested agencies, alternatives/design exceptions, ADA needs, ITS risk, technical studies, ROW/utilities, funding, schedule, who advertises/awards/administers and who maintains. It connects directly to scope, authorization and PS&E. See §7.1 and §7.6.

The review meeting's applicability differs from the required record. Section 7.2 distinguishes significant NHS, minor NHS, non-NHS, bridge-program and emergency cases. Section 7.5 still requires the field-review form for Federal-aid projects when a meeting is not required; emergency relief uses its damage-assessment form instead. A UI that marks all field-review work not applicable because no meeting is required would lose the record. The chapter calls for form preparation before or with PES submission, named attendees, preliminary maps/data, signed records, supplements for later answers and periodic review of changed scope. See §7.2–7.7.

There is a source tension to preserve. Chapter 6 printed page 32 broadly says field reviews are required for NHS projects, while Chapter 7 §7.2 provides a more specific Caltrans determination and minor/significant distinctions. Model the DLAE determination and relevant program rule; do not silently turn the broad sentence into a universal meeting requirement. Conversely, retain the required documentation even when the meeting is optional.

Existing project GIS, shared files, work plans and stage-gate evidence can host this. The California stage-gate template has a field-review disposition label but no full typed attendance/decision/supplement workflow was established in the sampled code. M10a/b and M5 should produce a reusable scoping/field-review packet, with separately owned decisions and geometry versions. Verify HBP versus minor NHS versus ordinary non-NHS and emergency cases, no-meeting documentation, changed footprint, unsigned supplement and external recipient reuse. No actual Caltrans participation is implied by an internally generated packet.

## Chapter 8. Public hearings

Source: [official chapter 8](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch08.pdf), all 8 PDF pages, printed contents i and 1–7; apply OB26-01 R1 as described above.

This chapter adds formal hearing administration to the engagement scope. Formal and open-forum formats both require a record. Necessity and a notice of opportunity for a hearing are separate decisions; an outstanding request cannot be treated as withdrawn merely because staff met with the requester. The chapter requires written withdrawal to support that path. See §8.1–8.3.

Notice publication needs channels, exact text/map/version, dates and evidence of publication or delivery. Electronic posting is one channel, not proof of every required notice. Public document availability, newspaper notices, hearing date, request deadline and post-hearing submissions are distinct clocks. Language access, affected-property outreach and public inspection/copying remain important. See §8.4–8.7. The funding needed for an actual required newspaper notice is a project operating cost; free OpenPlan cannot make third-party publication free.

The hearing record includes a title/contents, hearing summary, speaker index, verbatim transcript, displays, submitted documents, publicity/notices and invitations. An open forum also needs a synopsis of comments not entered in the formal transcript. The record is furnished to DLAE and must be revisited when a relevant project change calls for a rehearing. This is broader than a moderated comment export or AI summary. See §8.8–8.9.

An internal wording conflict needs explicit treatment. The notice provision at printed page 5 allows submissions until a stated date at least ten days after the hearing, while the hearing-procedure language at printed page 6 says no later than ten days, with a longer period for more complex/controversial proposals. Preserve both references and obtain a reviewed project-specific notice/closing date. Do not hardcode either phrase without that review or use a deadline engine to erase a received comment.

Current source `openplan/src/lib/engagement/comment-matrix.ts` describes its output as a staff cue and explicitly disclaims legal sufficiency. The inspected functions treat meeting/email sources as internal/private unless explicitly public, and hold unresolved duplicates from publication. Those can protect a public derivative but cannot define the authoritative hearing record: oral, written and email submissions and repeated comments need original retention and appropriate access. The existing California gate `G04_OUTREACH_PUBLIC_HEARING` names notice/package/record evidence, but the sampled template requires that bundle generally, rather than implementing case-specific hearing necessity, withdrawal, notice receipts, transcript custody and rehearing.

Extend M9 with formal notice/hearing/administrative-record workflows, joined to M8/M10b and M2/M4. Verify unresolved hearing request, unsigned withdrawal, changed notice, missing newspaper receipt, translated vital material, oral/open-forum evidence, incomplete transcript, late/comment-period changes and independent full-record export. Public publication still needs approval; an unmoderated private record is not automatically public. Observe an agency hearing clerk/planner and actual public participants, including language/access needs.

## Chapter 17. Project completion

Source: [official chapter 17](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch17.pdf), all 6 PDF pages, printed contents i and 1–5. The chapter is short, but its final packet depends on many prior records. State-administered contracts in State ROW follow the separate Construction Manual as described in §17.1.

The as-built obligation includes structure sheets, responsible engineer, acceptance date and contract identity, with corrections on the appropriate drawings. FROE includes final invoice and final detail estimate reconciliation, change-order summary even when none, acceptance, applicable structures/ROW/force-account records and the responsible public employee's signature. A final zero-dollar invoice is still called for when all funding has already been disbursed. An unresolved claim prevents treating its final estimate as settled. State-only and PE-only closeout use different packages. See §17.2–17.3.

Record physical completion, contract acceptance, last reimbursable phase, final cost/claim resolution, reimbursement submission, payment and federal closeout as distinct events. The chapter's six-month/lapse-year timing must be reconciled with Chapter 3's project-end-date and Chapter 5's invoice clocks, not implemented as one generic closeout due date. Preserve overpayments, corrections and potential recovery obligations. The chapter points to further consequences when discrepancies remain unresolved; software should surface the actual notice and responsible reviewer, not initiate offsets or repayment automatically. See §17.3 and §17.5.

OB26-03 explicitly suspends forms 17-F, 17-F1 and 17-O. Do not block current closeout for those suspended requirements or destroy previously filed forms. The retained prompt-payment/subcontract oversight duties and the current applicable final packet still need evidence. [Official OB26-03](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/ob/2026/ob26-03.pdf), PDF page 4.

Existing invoicing, project records, artifact packages and `G09_COMPLETION_MAINTENANCE_AUDIT` provide useful foundations, but a template title is not reconciled final cost or independently reusable as-builts. M10d/M11/M13 should complete federal construction, state-funded, PE-only, ROW and force-account variants as applicable. Verify zero-dollar final invoice, true no-change/no-claim assertions versus unknowns, a pending claim, fund substitution changing applicability, rejected final estimate, late closeout and retained maintenance obligations. An asset recipient should reopen the signed records without the original planner or server.

## Shared architecture and source cautions

The existing stage-gate summary in `openplan/src/lib/stage-gates/summary.ts:250–330` correctly refuses unscoped decisions, preserves unavailable reads as unknown and uses the recorded project decision. Its California template calls itself a scaffold and has nine broad gates. The inspected evidence type holds ID/title/type/required plus a conditional expression, but does not itself encode external approval receipt, authority, effective dates or revocation. Extend the existing decision/evidence architecture with typed case requirements and source-bound approvals; do not replace it with an AI determination or treat an uploaded document as a Caltrans decision.

The manual retains obsolete references, different review-period wording and some old operational media instructions. OB26-01 explicitly anticipates further revision. Actual deployment must refresh current SER forms, assigned authority, agency submission methods and the applicable statutory/regulatory requirements. All linked external manuals, underlying statutes and every exhibit were not read here. The latest forms index also lists a May 2026 PES pilot; its applicability must be checked separately from the ordinary form, and a browser-rendered placeholder must not be mistaken for the pilot's content.

These findings extend existing roadmap homes. They do not narrow national v1, require a new module for each Caltrans chapter or establish that any current workflow has passed professional acceptance.
