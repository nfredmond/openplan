# New York local-project procedures and the federal framework

Research date: September 4, 2026 Pacific / September 5 UTC. Selected-source reconnaissance; **not a complete New York manual review or operational compliance assessment**. No checkout, application, database, browser, account or worker changes.

New York is a strong early test of OpenPlan’s national capital-delivery architecture. It shares the Federal-aid lifecycle with California, but uses different agreements, responsible officials, forms, review routes and retention rules. Build a reusable project/evidence/financial core with verified authority profiles. A California checklist with renamed agencies would fail this test.

## Shared federal requirements, different administration

FHWA Order 5020.2 explains that a state transportation agency remains accountable for applicable Federal-aid requirements when it subawards delivery to a local agency. States have flexibility in their oversight strategies; FHWA checks their oversight. Adequate project-delivery systems and accounting controls are central. The order is dated 2014 and still linked by the current LPA resource page; its old regulatory numbering and historical assessment dates should not become hardcoded current rules. [Order 5020.2 ¶¶4–8](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/legsregs/directives/orders/50202.cfm), [LPA resources](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/federalaid/lpa/).

The current national stewardship directory provides a separate agreement for each state/DC and Puerto Rico. New York’s 2025 agreement distinguishes assumable approvals, retained FHWA decisions, NHS/non-NHS, and project-specific oversight plans. Attachment A governs delegated actions; NYSDOT remains responsible for subrecipient capacity and controls. The non-NHS section expressly recognizes a qualifying locally adopted, FHWA-recognized roadway guide. Consequently the 2011 chapter’s broad state-design mandate and 2018 chapter’s old oversight thresholds cannot be treated as the whole current rule. [National directory](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/federalaid/stewardship/index.cfm), [NY agreement §§I–VII, pp1–6](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/federalaid/stewardship/agreements/ny.pdf).

**Engineering implication:** select authority from recipient/pass-through relationship, program, ownership, roadway system, project-specific agreement and effective date. Bind state/local/federal obligations independently. A grant directly awarded to a municipality may have a different administration path from state-apportioned funds; FHWA’s directory separately links its Title 23 applicability dashboard for non-state discretionary recipients. “Federally funded” alone is insufficient to select LAPM or LPM.

## Differences that should change the implementation

**Management and design roles.** New York identifies the Regional Local Project Liaison (RLPL), Responsible Local Official (RLO), sponsor PM and EIC/RE. A jointly developed PMP assigns activities, prerequisites, signers and schedules, with revisions affecting the relevant signatories. Ordinary workspace permission does not establish those authorities. Where state specifications refer to NYSDOT reviews, the actual sponsor design-professional/materials role must be identified rather than blindly copied. On/off-system and individual project segments affect responsibilities. [Chapter 2 §2.10, pp14–16](https://www.dot.ny.gov/portal/pls/portal/MEXIS_APP.PA_PLAFAP_ADMIN.show_file?id=3681), [Chapter 9 §§9.1–9.2, pp3–4](https://www.dot.ny.gov/portal/pls/portal/MEXIS_APP.PA_PLAFAP_ADMIN.show_file?id=2890).

**Phase authorization and agreements.** The current Chapter 3 separates preliminary/final design and ROW incidentals/acquisition. Sponsor-executed State-Local Agreement (SLA), federal authorization, state execution and reimbursement availability are distinct events. Schedule A contains phase ceilings; changing scope/cost/schedule can require a supplemental SLA. This supports the proposed authorization ledger and rules out one undifferentiated project “approved” status. [Chapter 3 §3.4, pp10–11](https://www.dot.ny.gov/portal/pls/portal/MEXIS_APP.PA_PLAFAP_ADMIN.show_file?id=5425).

**Reimbursement and retention.** Chapter 5 uses FIN 426LL/427LL/428LL and certification, organized by nine-digit PINs under an SLA, with current/prior/life-to-date costs. Standard forms must remain unaltered. The RLPL review, BSC processing, SFS receipt and payment are distinct; direct sponsor submission to BSC has its own notification/review path. Final reimbursement closes the entire SLA/all PINs, not just one phase, and needs NYS 1446LA. Corrections preserve prior-request linkage. The specified financed-asset/ownership/disposition records require 36 years after NYSDOT final payment under the SLA. These are concrete differences from California forms and a generic three-year timer. [Chapter 5 §§5.3–5.4, pp5–19](https://www.dot.ny.gov/portal/pls/portal/MEXIS_APP.PA_PLAFAP_ADMIN.show_file?id=5365).

**Construction.** Chapter 15 requires a NYSDOT-signed CMP before award, updated approval before work, and a QMP within it; staff changes longer than a week can trigger revision. It recognizes equivalent automated recordkeeping, with approval of alternate procedures—not automatic acceptance of any software. All change orders receive NYSDOT review; significant changes require prework approval. Contractor extension and SLA no-cost extension are separate. General federal retention and holds coexist with Marchiselli’s36-year rule, anchored to later construction acceptance/final payment. Bridge opening notifications, material certifications, load-rating/as-built information and closed uncompleted-work agreements feed closeout. [Chapter 15 §§15.3–15.6, pp9–12,17–20,33–37](https://www.dot.ny.gov/portal/pls/portal/MEXIS_APP.PA_PLAFAP_ADMIN.show_file?id=5430).

**Design inference:** retain the same project identity, financial/source-cost ledger, signed evidence revisions and workflow engine. Supply New York role routing, PIN/SLA hierarchy, approved procedures, source forms and retention conditions through reviewed profiles. Existing project phases and reimbursement descriptors are appropriate starting points; they do not establish these controls today. Current roadmap M10/M11/M14 already provide homes. Add New York validation to their definitions of done, not a parallel application.

## Tribal and territorial authority are separate branches

Tribal governments must not inherit state administrative authority from a polygon intersection. FHWA’s Office of Tribal Transportation describes direct agreements and transfers under which stewardship lies with OTT or BIA. Determine the particular recipient, agreement, program and transfer conditions; cooperation with a state does not turn a Tribe into its subdivision. The full Tribal Transportation Program Delivery Guide still needs a separate reading. [FHWA Tribal Finance](https://highways.fhwa.dot.gov/federal-lands/tribal/finance), [technical-assistance roles](https://highways.fhwa.dot.gov/iija/technical-assistance-local-support).

The April 2026 Territorial Highway Program guidance covers American Samoa, CNMI, Guam and USVI. Its territory-specific agreement identifies applicable Title 23 provisions, standards, maintenance and oversight; agreements require review at least every two years. Some ordinary state-program provisions are excluded. Puerto Rico is a separate highway-program branch, not interchangeable with these four territories. This is an architectural requirement and disclosure boundary, not a claim this reconnaissance implements territorial coverage. [THP guidance §§F–I, printed pp4–6](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/specialfunding/thp/260330.pdf), [program overview](https://highways.fhwa.dot.gov/iija/fact-sheets/territorial-and-puerto-rico-highway-program).

## Recommended next step and verification

Use California and New York permitted project files to prove the common architecture before scaling procedural profiles. Keep all 50 states/DC as the binding destination. National inventory should record each manual’s current directory, chapters, forms, bulletins, stewardship agreement, known conflicts and reviewed coverage; “source found” must remain different from “workflow validated.”

For the New York case, demonstrate separate PIN phases under one SLA, corrected reimbursements, a CMP staff change, contractor/SLA time extensions and applicable 36-year records. Mutations should expose wrong state forms, wrong reviewer, premature whole-SLA finalization and an incorrectly shortened retention date. A harmless display-label change should survive. Require a practicing sponsor/RLPL reviewer and finance specialist to assess the packet; no agent simulation or green schema test establishes administrative acceptance.

Operating costs are source maintenance, secure long-term storage, restoration and agency review. Existing accounting/construction systems should exchange reviewed records with OpenPlan; no paid Appia, Primavera or other system is required merely because a manual names it. Exact contemporary DBE/BABA instructions, all remaining chapters/forms, actual agreements and human acceptance remain unreviewed prerequisites before New York production claims.
