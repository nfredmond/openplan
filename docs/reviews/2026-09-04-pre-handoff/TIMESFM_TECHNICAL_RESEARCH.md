## What the models do

TimesFM 2.5 forecasts numeric series from their histories. Its 200M-parameter architecture uses 32-step input patches and 128-step output patches, with an optional 1024-step quantile head. The advertised 16384-step context needs qualification: the current PyTorch compiler enforces rounded context plus horizon within 16384, and continuous quantiles require horizon at most 1024. These are computational bounds, not defensible transportation planning horizons. [Definition](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/src/timesfm/timesfm_2p5/timesfm_2p5_base.py#L84), [compiler checks](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/src/timesfm/timesfm_2p5/timesfm_2p5_torch.py#L377).

2.5 adds covariates through a separate linear regression combined with TimesFM residual forecasts. It accepts static and dynamic numeric/categorical inputs; dynamic inputs extend through the forecast horizon. Future weather or economic conditions would need their own available-at-forecast-time values or explicitly named assumptions. This does not establish causal effects of a road project. [Covariate implementation](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/src/timesfm/timesfm_2p5/timesfm_2p5_base.py#L198).

3.0 introduces native multivariate, past-only and past/future covariates. However, its benchmark evaluator allows 32 combined variates per forward pass, subsamples excess covariates and splits target channels into chunks. Cross-channel modeling therefore does not mean unlimited joint-network inference. Its default postprocessing includes quantile sorting and positivity handling. [3.0 evaluator](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/src/timesfm3/evaluator.py#L29). Its weight-license restriction remains decisive for deployment.

## Consequential adapter risks

The 2.5 forecast path automatically removes leading NaNs and interpolates remaining gaps. Tests explicitly document that the all-NaN helper returns the original array despite a docstring claiming an empty result. This is source inspection, not a reproduced runtime result. OpenPlan should reject empty/all-missing inputs, distinguish missing from zero, retain timestamps and observed/imputed masks, and preregister permitted gaps. Never let preprocessing manufacture apparent observed traffic. [Forecast preprocessing](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/src/timesfm/timesfm_2p5/timesfm_2p5_base.py#L33), [upstream missing-data tests](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/tests/test_base_utils.py#L72).

The 2.5 PyTorch wrapper returns channel 5 as its point forecast, which corresponds to the median in its mean-plus-nine-quantiles tensor. The model card separately describes the tensor's first channel as mean. An adapter must label the actual statistic, preserve quantile levels and record positivity/crossing adjustments. Quantile ordering does not demonstrate calibrated coverage for a particular geography or horizon. [Return value and adjustments](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/src/timesfm/timesfm_2p5/timesfm_2p5_torch.py#L473).

## Scientific and operating limits

The cited ICML 2024 paper covers the original model. Its architecture and limitations sections discuss point forecasting, covariates and interpretability; they do not validate the newly released 3.0 model. Its results concern forecasting benchmarks. They do not establish trip generation, mode choice, network assignment, causal intervention effects or nationwide transportation validity. [Paper v4, §§3–5 and Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10688v4).

The 2.5 card lists GiftEvalPretrain, Wikipedia through November 2023, Google Trends through 2022, and synthetic/augmented data. Those dated sources do not establish a single global training cutoff. The 3.0 card says overlapping fev-bench datasets were excluded, but that is not proof that OpenPlan's proposed test series were unseen. Record training overlap as unknown until source-level lineage is checked. [2.5 training description](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch/blob/1d952420fba87f3c6dee4f240de0f1a0fbc790e3/README.md), [3.0 training description](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch/blob/43046b85ec22d584a13f8098c2ed39c889e129c2/README.md).

The 2.5 implementation selects CUDA when available, otherwise CPU. Source availability establishes neither acceptable latency nor memory fit on Nathaniel's computer. PyTorch and the optional XReg/JAX dependencies need a pinned environment; unrestricted upstream minimum versions are insufficient reproducibility. Measure cold/warm latency, peak RAM/VRAM and interruption recovery later in an isolated worker. No current hardware-performance claim is justified. [Device selection](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/src/timesfm/timesfm_2p5/timesfm_2p5_torch.py#L71), [dependencies](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm/blob/0df95ae62085a6ac0d0afd1ad40dee2e6c1356ab/pyproject.toml).

An appropriate first experiment would compare short-horizon count or ridership forecasts against seasonal-naive and simple statistical baselines, using rolling forecast origins, untouched temporal evaluation data, geographic subgroup results and empirical interval coverage. Freeze preprocessing, horizons and metrics before evaluation. Preserve failures and inconclusive outcomes; do not use forecasts as observed validation counts or change AequilibraE/ActivitySim acceptance boundaries. This is my proposed experiment, not evidence of suitability.

## Assessment of the answer Nathaniel supplied

The supplied answer correctly identifies temporal forecasting and covariates as potential transportation applications, but its suitability and accuracy language is stronger than the reviewed evidence. Zero-shot means no task-specific training, not no evaluation. Adding holidays, closures or weather does not guarantee an improvement; a never-before-observed closure or new facility can require a structural/causal model. Forecasting a volume also does not itself optimize routing, tolls, signal control or service frequency.

The supplied worst-case characterization of the 90th percentile is incorrect: even a well-calibrated marginal forecast would exceed that level about 10% of the time. Separate marginal station/time quantiles also do not define the worst joint network peak. Patching reduces computation but supplies no hardware-fit guarantee. The current evaluator's 32 combined-channel batching limits qualify the whole-network claim. Those limitations and the 3.0 production-license restriction are material to OpenPlan.

The initial recommendation, superseded by the later 3.0-only decision above, was a bounded optional 2.5 experiment, with any operational integration contingent on its measured value and licensed deployability. This could expand OpenPlan's forecasting capabilities while leaving separate demand-model development and validation intact. See draft roadmap S2a.
