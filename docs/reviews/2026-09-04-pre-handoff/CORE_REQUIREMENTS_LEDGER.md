## Project management through a planner's work

The proposed controls adapt established practice to professional planning services. GAO's cost guide supports defined scope, work breakdown, stated assumptions, risk analysis and estimates updated with actual costs; it expressly covers capital and noncapital projects. This informs the design rather than creating an OpenPlan compliance certification. [GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide, 2020](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-195g).

Cost forecasts must account for schedule effects. Linked activities and approved dates help explain how delay changes delivery and cost. This supports retaining a baseline and showing a forecast with its dependencies, rather than displaying isolated due dates. [GAO Schedule Assessment Guide, 2015](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-16-89g).

The following are engineering recommendations for implementing Nathaniel's outcome, not quotations or universal statutory requirements:

1. **Begin with the agreed work.** Bind executed scope, fee basis and authorized ceiling to a modest task structure, named deliverables, owners, acceptance criteria and deadlines. Support fixed fee, time-and-materials, not-to-exceed, on-call/task-order and mixed arrangements explicitly. Keep contract fee, internal cost budget and funding award distinct. A small study should need only a few tasks and a weekly update.
2. **Keep the original promise and approved changes.** Preserve original and current authorized hours, fees and dates, plus proposed amendments. A PM may revise an estimate without rewriting the contract. Changes need source authority, effective date, reason, approver and cost/schedule effects. Record already-incurred overrun work truthfully even when further commitments require approval.
3. **Record effort once and retain its allocation.** Staff time and direct/subconsultant costs link to the correct contract/task and relevant deliverable allocations. Person, labor role, billing rate and internal cost rate are different facts. Effective rates and corrections retain history. Rollups by employee, task and deliverable are views of the same source amounts, not numbers to add together again.
4. **Ask what finishing will take.** The PM updates remaining hours/cost by task and person or role, with assumptions and an as-of date. Forecast cost at completion uses compatible actual cost plus estimated remaining cost, counting outstanding commitments once. Compare forecast with the approved budget on that same basis. Show missing rates, stale time and incomplete estimates; never convert them to zero.
5. **Model planning's work and waiting.** Separate staff work from data delivery, field season, internal technical review, client comment rounds, consultation, public notice, board packet cutoff, hearing/adoption and revision. Use applicable working calendars, dependencies and resource availability. Preserve approved, forecast and actual dates. Draft completion, client acceptance and formal adoption are distinct milestones.
6. **Show an actionable warning.** Explain which predecessor, unavailable reviewer, additional alternative or repeated review round threatens fee or deadline. Link an owner, next action and decision date to the existing risk/issue/decision records. Show what an amendment or reassignment would change without assuming it is authorized.
7. **Reconcile money without changing its meaning.** Keep internal costs, gross billings, retention, credits, client receipts, eligible funding costs, claims and funder remittances separate and linked. Mark basis, currency, as-of date and completeness in every summary/export. An invoice sent is not cash received; invoiced or spent percentage does not establish accepted progress.
8. **Keep routine use proportionate.** Give staff a quick work-entry path and the PM a concise weekly review. Add resource forecasts, approvals and funder detail where the case requires them. Formal earned-value measures may be useful for a contract that specifies them; do not require an enterprise EVMS to manage a small planning assignment or invent earned value from spending.

## First observed project-management outcome

A planning PM opens a permitted corridor or land-use study, reconciles its agreement and opening balances, reviews current work by staff/task/deliverable, sees that another client comment round threatens the agreed fee and hearing date, and records a defensible response. A finance reviewer reconstructs the same figures, and a staff member records work without gaining access to other employees' confidential rates. A capital planning/design case then verifies the contract-to-funder connection separately.

Use realistic but clearly labeled test fixtures for automated financial edge cases. Human observation must use an authorized real or sanitized case with declared limitations. No such live study was performed in this review. Human input still needed concerns real agreements, approval responsibilities, working vocabulary and the first observation cases; engineering choices about attribution, arithmetic and historical integrity are the technical lead's responsibility.

## Local tax measures and administered grants

Nathaniel reaffirmed municipal reporting and program administration on September 4. The local-measure history report recovers the August 11 request for public tax revenue administration and sub-agency reports, alongside separately required post-award grant administration. Fine-grained physical-output requirements are binding from the current instruction regardless of whether their earlier wording is recovered.

Keep fund receipts, programmed amounts, allocations, commitments, recipient expenditures, requests, approvals and actual disbursements distinct. Use the program's actual terms for formula distributions, advances or reimbursements. A report can be due without a payment request. Tax-rate labels and measure letters are not program identities; place, adopting body, election/ordinance and effective version identify the obligation, and successor measures preserve inherited liabilities and reporting history.

Metrics need a defined activity and unit, reporting period, project/segment, source, cumulative-versus-period basis and review state. Physical totals and funding-attributed totals are different views. A city must be able to report its own work without broad administration powers; authorized reviewers and the public receive the appropriate records, with confidential drafts and attachments protected. Reuse these reporting/approval components for grants, while preserving each source's actual award requirements.
