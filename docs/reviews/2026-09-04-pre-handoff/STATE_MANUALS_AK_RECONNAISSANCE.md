## Recommendation

Prioritize Alaska as an early contrasting acceptance case. Its most useful distinction is an **award-specific division of ownership, administration, approval, funding and maintenance**, particularly for Denali Commission projects and separate Tribal funding paths. A state label or generic local-agency role cannot determine these responsibilities. Seasonal suspension also provides a concrete test of schedule, maintenance custody and condition evidence.

The first task is authority reconciliation. Alaska posts a Local Agency Procedures Manual explicitly labeled **Draft Version**. Later guidance and executed agreements must be checked before any of its rules becomes an operational default.

## Selected findings

### Delivery and agreements

[Draft overview](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcslocalagency/assets/pdf/manual/ch02_overview_sw.pdf), §§2.1–2.3, printed pp. 2-1 through 2-3, ties responsibility to facility ownership, municipal road powers, title/maintenance commitments, STIP, legislative authority and executed agreements. It also contains restrictive language about DOT&PF management and staffing by named municipalities. Later [HPCM §420.4](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcsprecon/assets/pdf/preconhwy/chapters/chapter4.pdf), printed p. 420-3, states DOT&PF may permit LPAs to exercise assumed responsibilities while remaining accountable. Treat the older restriction as unresolved historical guidance, not an exclusion to hardcode into OpenPlan.

[Draft Chapter 4](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcslocalagency/assets/pdf/manual/ch04_agreement.pdf), §§4.1–4.4, requires an agreement defining work, agency contributions, funding, matching conditions and title transfer, with amendments for changed scope, responsibilities or funding. Municipal agreements and state-agency Reimbursable Services Agreements differ. Preserve agreement identity, execution, amendment and each actor's actual role rather than one “project owner” field.

### Funding, invoicing and closure

[Construction Chapter 2](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcsconst/assets/pdf/constman/2022/ch2.pdf), §§2.1–2.3, pp. 2-1 through 2-3, distinguishes federal project identifiers, state project accounts, Project Development Authorization, phase authority and encumbrance. Contract award and subsequent changes require sufficient phase funding and separate funding-category encumbrances. FAA and FHWA agreements have different administration; do not copy the chapter's dated FAA thresholds into a current rule without verification.

[Draft billing Chapter 6](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcslocalagency/assets/pdf/manual/ch06_progressbilling.pdf), §§6.1–6.3, pp. 6-1 through 6-2, distinguishes state-agency RSA entries, municipal MOA reimbursement and Transfer of Responsibility Agreement billing. The agreement may also require the locality to pay DOT&PF for match/nonparticipating expenses. Money can move in either direction. The chapter's obsolete OMB Circular A-87 reference demonstrates why source currency must be resolved before implementing cost eligibility.

[Construction Chapter 16](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcsconst/assets/pdf/constman/2022/ch16.pdf), §§16.1–16.4 and 16.13–16.15, separates physical completion, contractor release/final estimate, final acceptance, financial phase closure and federal voucher. Financial closure requires liquidated encumbrances, completed relevant audits and no remaining charges. The final report combines quantities, funding eligibility, materials certification, changes, claims and as-builts. Do not equate spending the award with closure. Its retention section names different triggering events and contains dated regulation references; record triggers/holds but verify current authority before allowing destruction.

### Denali Commission and state-only paths

[HPCM §§490 and 495](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcsprecon/assets/pdf/preconhwy/chapters/chapter4.pdf), printed pp. 490-1 and 495-1 through 495-3, distinguish state-funded projects from Denali Commission work. Denali guidance describes three DOT&PF involvement levels: administration; design/procure/monitor; and cost accounting/periodic inspection. The individual grant can refine that responsibility matrix.

For the described Denali path, the Commission retains environmental approval, while ROW/utilities and maintenance responsibilities depend on administration and ownership. The chapter describes a different authorization/programming route from FHWA projects. Implement explicit award conditions and authorized decisions, not a universal requirement that every federally connected Alaska project pass an FHWA gate or appear in STIP. This is the manual's described path; the actual current Commission award/MOA still needs inspection before operational use.

### Tribal and remote context

DOT&PF's [Tribal collaboration guidance](https://dot.alaska.gov/tribalrelations/tools.shtml) describes transfers to the Tribal Transportation Program, unique MOA/MOU tracking and rural post-award conferences. Its transfer explanation contains inconsistent statutory numbering, so eligible sources/exclusions need confirmation against the actual transfer authority. Its SharePoint templates were not accessed. Government-to-government consultation, agency coordination, public participation, Tribal governments and ANCSA corporations need distinct identities and permissions.

The [Tribal Relations page](https://dot.alaska.gov/tribalrelations/) describes the 2002 policy update as draft, with Tribal review March 27–May 11, 2026 and further internal review planned. Do not label that draft adopted. The [FHWA TTP Delivery Guide page](https://highways.dot.gov/federal-lands/tribal/guide), updated January 15, 2026, expressly concerns Tribes with or considering an FHWA TTP agreement; its detailed guide was only inventoried here. A Tribe's applicable delivery authority must not be treated as merely a local-agency delegation.

[Construction §14.3](https://dot.alaska.gov/stwddes/dcsconst/assets/pdf/constman/2022/ch14.pdf), printed p. 14-1, requires written seasonal suspension/resumption and condition documentation, with maintenance responsibility set by the contract. Calendar-day counting follows specified contract periods. Weather alone is not automatic time-extension authority. For remote operation, saved field drafts and resumable attachment transfer are recommended acceptance conditions, not a manual-derived connectivity mandate.
