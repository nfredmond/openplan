# Capital delivery through construction and closeout: priority research

**Research date:** September 4, 2026. **Scope:** California local capital delivery, including an RTPA helping administer bridge projects, with reusable nationwide architecture. **Disposition:** research and proposed design only. No implementation, roadmap change, submission or operational action is authorized by this report.

## Recommendation

Make **one capital project carried from programming through construction, reimbursement and accepted closeout** the next coherent product outcome. Extend Projects, its delivery records and stage gates, Invoicing, funding awards and evidence bundles. These already provide a usable structural starting point. A new independent construction-management module would duplicate that spine before its existing parts are connected.

The missing outcome is not a larger checklist. An agency needs to establish who may act, which money may pay for which phase, what work was accepted, what the agency paid, what it claimed, what Caltrans reimbursed, and what remains unresolved. Those answers must survive a changed estimate, a partial payment, a rejected invoice, a staff handoff and a final audit. Construction completion, contractor settlement, reimbursement completion and funder closeout must remain separate events.

California should be the first deeply validated delivery profile, without making California geography sufficient evidence of LAPM applicability. Retain all-state/DC v1 scope, California depth, the existing planning/environmental work, and separate scientific validation of both demand methods. This priority adds continuity through delivery; it does not redefine v1 around a smaller capital-management product.

## 1. Bind the funding and authority before producing requirements

For each project, record the asset owner, sponsor, implementing/administering agency, agreement signatory and payee, program administrator, responsible public employee, project manager, resident engineer/structure representative, consultant, contractor, finance reviewer and external reviewer. An RTPA's involvement does not make those roles interchangeable.

For federal-aid work, LAPM §2.12.2 says the employee in responsible charge must be employed directly by the local public agency receiving federal funds; a consultant cannot hold that role. That public employee need not be an engineer. Do not confuse this role with engineering licensure or the construction resident engineer's separate qualifications. **Design implication:** workspace owner/admin permission cannot stand in for statutory or contractual signature authority. [LAPM Chapter 2](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch02.pdf)

| Funding/administration case | Applicable starting authority | Required product distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Local-only capital project, off the State Highway System (SHS) | Agency procurement, public works rules, executed contracts, CEQA/permits and applicable state law; no automatic federal-aid checklist | Select a local delivery profile. Do not require an E-76 or label the file LAPM-ready solely because the project is in California. |
| State-only funding administered through Local Assistance | Program/award conditions, current CTC guidelines, LAPG Chapter 25 and the State-Only Funded Matrix | Store program, allocation, expenditure/award/completion deadlines and approved exceptions separately. A state-only project is not a federal project with the federal label hidden. [LAPG 25](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapg/g25.pdf), [SOF matrix](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/reports/state-only-funded-matrix-20191028.pdf) |
| FHWA funds passed through Caltrans; locally administered off SHS | LAPM, project agreements, authorization and program-specific LAPG guidance, with current bulletins | Bind the actual authorized phases and funding shares. Track DLAE routing and accepted submissions. [LAPM](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/local-assistance/guidelines-and-procedures/local-assistance-procedures-manual-lapm) |
| Local agency administers work on SHS | Cooperative agreement, Caltrans oversight, Construction Manual local-agency supplement and structure guidance | Agency provides the construction team; Caltrans provides independent quality assurance/oversight. Do not apply every Construction Manual duty to every off-SHS project. [Construction Manual §9-2](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/construction/construction-manual/section-9-2-projects-adminstered-by-a-local-agency-on-the-state-highway-system) |
| Caltrans administers construction on state right of way | Caltrans Construction Manual and the agreement defining local participation | Track the agency's commitments, contributions, reviews and acceptance interfaces without pretending it is the resident engineer administering the contract. [LAPM 17, §17.1](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch17.pdf) |
| Mixed funding, direct federal awards, FTA transfers or special programs | Each award's own authority, pass-through/direct relationship and program conditions | Use separate funding lines and explicit applicability review. Do not force every award into LAPM 5-A. Direct federal/FTA and emergency-delivery profiles need additional program research before release. |
| RTPA coordinates, programs or provides management for another agency's bridge | The above funding/administration case plus the documented interagency arrangement | Keep regional programming, asset ownership, agreement/payee authority and delegated management separate; do not infer authority from the RTPA label. |

**Bridge-specific distinction:** LAPG Chapter 6's HBP path is for qualifying locally owned bridges outside SHS; its “on-system/off-system” funding distinction concerns the **federal-aid system**, not whether the bridge lies on SHS. Its eligibility/application process uses bridge inventory and inspection evidence, with DLAE and Structures Local Assistance roles. Store the bridge identifier, owner, inspection source/date, scope eligibility, programmed phase/cost, authorization and approved scope/cost/schedule changes. Do not assume every RTPA bridge project uses HBP, or hardcode one reimbursement percentage across bridge projects. [LAPG 6, §§6.1–6.3 and 6.8](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapg/g06.pdf)

A requirements binding should therefore include funding source/program and award vintage; direct/pass-through status; SHS and federal-aid-system status; administering agency; delivery/procurement method; phase; applicable agreement/authorization; relevant environmental/permit authorities; construction authorization and advertisement/award dates; source revisions and approved deviations. Unknown values leave the affected requirement unresolved.

The procedural basis for these phase boundaries is not merely an inferred “standard project schedule”:

- **Authorization:** LAPM §3.3 distinguishes PE, ROW/utility and construction/CE. Construction authorization precedes advertisement; CE must be specifically authorized. PE exceptions exist, including conditioned at-risk PE, so a blanket “nothing before authorization is ever eligible” rule is wrong. Preserve the exact exception basis and effective reimbursement date. [LAPM 3](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch03.pdf)
- **Agreements:** identify the applicable Master Agreement, project/Program Supplement and covenants rather than using a general “award accepted” checkbox. [LAPM 4](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch04.pdf)
- **Procurement:** consultant selection and financial/contract administration have a dedicated A&E procedure. An engagement record or generic low-bid workflow is not a substitute. Procurement method, evaluation and cost negotiation need their own source-backed record. [LAPM 10](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch10.pdf)
- **PS&E:** estimates distinguish contract items, nonparticipating work, agency-furnished materials, supplemental work, contingencies and CE. These are useful structured cost dimensions, not one undifferentiated project amount. [LAPM 12, §12.12](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch12.pdf)
- **Advertisement/award:** LAPM §15.2 distinguishes significant NHS projects' approved administration procedures from other projects' checklist route. Record the determination rather than choosing a path from project size alone. [LAPM 15](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch15.pdf)
- **Construction:** source records must support supervision, testing, conformance and payment. LAPM §16.13 connects pay quantities back to measurements and force-account costs and requires accounting support for contingencies and final cost. A PDF uploaded at the end cannot reconstruct missing contemporaneous evidence. [LAPM 16, §§16.3 and 16.13](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch16.pdf)

## 3. Current policy changes make versioned applicability essential

The January LAPM is not the whole current rule set. Its official index points to superseding bulletins; the exhibits page was updated August 18, 2026. Store source URL, edition/revision, effective interval, retrieval date, cited section, superseding source and reviewer. Changes should reopen affected applicability decisions without silently rewriting historical packets. [LAPM index](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/local-assistance/guidelines-and-procedures/local-assistance-procedures-manual-lapm), [current exhibits](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/local-assistance/forms/local-assistance-procedures-manual-forms)

Four material examples found in this review:

1. **DBE:** OB26-03 says contract goals and goal-based monitoring/enforcement remain suspended pending the specified certification/program approvals. It changes chapter/exhibit treatment; the forms index marks numerous older DBE exhibits inactive and lists updated 9-P and 10-R documents. Do not instantiate January's DBE checklist unchanged, and do not infer that all civil-rights, bidder-list or prompt-payment duties disappeared. Determine the applicable contract/date treatment with the DLAE/DBE reviewer. [OB26-03](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/ob/2026/ob26-03.pdf)
2. **Buy America:** OB26-04's manufactured-product Phase 2 applies to construction E-76 authorization on/after **October 1, 2026**. It adds the greater-than-55-percent component-cost requirement to final US assembly, subject to the bulletin's applicability and exclusions. That future trigger must not be retroactively imposed on every existing contract or treated as already effective on this research date. [OB26-04](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/ob/2026/ob26-04.pdf)
3. **Environmental scope:** OB26-01 R1 changes the NEPA treatment/terminology of specified subjects while expressly retaining CEQA applicability for climate/GHG analysis. Model separate legal tracks and preserve underlying evidence; do not remove relevant California/local analysis because a federal document's scope differs. Exact project application needs the environmental lead's current review. [OB26-01 R1](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/ob/2026/ob26-01r1.pdf)

4. **Conflicting audit summaries:** the currently linked LAPG 25 §25.6.1 retains a $750,000/receipts-era audit summary, while LAPM 20 states $1 million of agency-wide federal expenditures. The current eCFR §200.501 page could not be retrieved in this session. Do not let a recently downloaded manual silently decide the conflict; the primary-source follow-up below establishes the fiscal-year transition. [LAPG 25](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapg/g25.pdf), [LAPM 20](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch20.pdf)

**Root follow-up, independently checked against the primary source:** the Federal Audit Clearinghouse specifies a $750,000-or-more threshold for fiscal years starting before October 1, 2024, and $1,000,000 or more for fiscal years starting on or after that date. This resolves the transition left open by the unsuccessful eCFR retrieval; it does not convert receipts into expenditures or make the test project-specific. Record the entity fiscal-year start and agency-wide federal expenditures, and refer interpretive questions, including special expenditure treatment, to the appropriate federal oversight/cognizant agency and finance/audit reviewer. [FAC: Is my organization required to conduct a Single Audit?](https://support.fac.gov/hc/en-us/articles/18792372809101-Is-my-organization-required-to-conduct-a-Single-Audit)

Do not turn this research into legal boilerplate. The current office bulletin, executed agreement, program instructions and responsible authority determine the actual project packet. A “not applicable” decision needs its basis and human owner; an unknown source revision must stay unresolved.

## 5. Incremental design inside existing modules

The following is a proposed design, not an implemented capability or a legal requirement in itself.

### A. Project delivery and authority binding

Add a project-level delivery profile with funding-line/phase overrides. Give every applicability determination one of `applies`, `does_not_apply`, `unresolved` or `superseded`, with basis, human reviewer and source version. Represent agency roles separately from login roles and retain delegated authority documents. A district label is entered/resolved from the project relationship, never inferred as D3 for all California work.

Keep planning milestones and contractual obligations distinct. Record actual deadline anchors—allocation, phase authorization, advertisement, award, acceptance, expenditure, final voucher—rather than treating award date plus a generic offset as legal timing. Existing work-plan offsets can remain editable scheduling aids with their existing caveats.

### B. Extend delivery records into contract administration

Attach a structured contract/work package to the existing project: executed scope, procurement record, baseline estimate, bid schedule, parties, authorized value and contract time. Add revisioned bid items/quantities, RFIs/submittals, daily records and tested-material references, potential claims and approved changes. A change records technical justification, cost/time effects, funding eligibility, environmental/permit implications and the required approving authorities.

Keep a working draft editable. Freeze the reviewed baseline or submission as a versioned record with the evidence it actually used; later corrections create revisions. Field/mobile capture should preserve author and observation time, sync state and duplicate protection. Photos and aerial products support observations; they do not automatically establish accepted quantities, engineering certification or payment entitlement.

### C. One cost identity through three financial directions

Preserve separate ledgers for (1) commitments/agency costs and vendor payments, (2) claims to a funder and remittances from it, and (3) consultancy client receivables. Link them by stable source-cost identifiers rather than summing them as independent expenses.

A cost line carries project, contract/task/bid item, phase, service date, quantity/rate/unit, source document, agency payment references, participating/nonparticipating allocation, funding split and authorization basis. Retain the approved pro-rata or fixed/lump-sum reimbursement basis, ceilings and effective amendments rather than applying one statewide share. Use decimal/cents arithmetic; retain the rate/estimate revision. Support partial payments, withholding, retention release, disallowance, credit/reversal and resubmission without overwriting prior amounts.

A claim line references eligible cost allocations. Record package draft/internal review, approved-for-submission, transmitted, received, returned, approved, partially reimbursed and settled as distinct events. External reference/date and accepted amount come from actual evidence, not a locally clicked status. Unknown payment dates or unresolved receipts remain unknown. Reject double allocation/duplicate claim of the same cost beyond its eligible amount, while allowing explicitly documented corrections.

Use database-side aggregates or complete pagination for financial totals, with explicit unreadable/incomplete states. Test beyond existing caps. A form summary, project ledger and award drawdown must reconcile to the same underlying cost/payment/claim facts.

### D. Official packet preparation, without false submission

The current worksheet should remain an internal reconciliation artifact. LAPM Chapter 5 requires LAPM 5-A with its billing summary/checklist and says CLPA no longer accepts LPA-created invoices; reimbursement is generally for costs the agency has already paid. Requirements include the agreement/authorization basis and award-package prerequisite. Monthly submission and the minimum six-month activity interval have stated context and exceptions. **Therefore:** retain official source forms and revisions; map reviewed data into the applicable original form and indexed attachments, then visually/arithmetically compare the output. Do not relabel OpenPlan's HTML worksheet as 5-A. [LAPM 5, §§5.2–5.5](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch05.pdf)

Official fillable forms may require a specific desktop reader; the exhibits site warns against opening some directly in-browser. Compatibility, field mapping, calculation behavior, signatures and acceptable output still need testing with the actual form and district reviewer. A useful first increment may be a reviewed field-mapping/export companion with the official form attached, explicitly “not ready to submit,” until faithful output is proved. No automated email/portal submission or claim of Caltrans acceptance follows from generation. [Forms index](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/local-assistance/forms/local-assistance-procedures-manual-forms)

### E. Closeout, handover and retained records

Create separate construction acceptance, final contractor settlement, final reimbursement, funder acceptance and award-balance reconciliation records. Support successful underspend with approved release/return of the balance. Carry unresolved claims/punch-list conditions explicitly. Handover includes approved record drawings, asset/bridge identity and continuing obligations, linked to the existing project/evidence library.

Under the covered LAPM federal-aid path, FROE timing is generally six months from project/last federally reimbursable phase completion, with the stated April 1 lapsing-year condition. The final invoice must match the final detail estimate; a zero final invoice can still be required. Structure completion has record-drawing and 17-J requirements. These are distinct from a “paid equals award” test. [LAPM 17, §§17.2–17.3](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch17.pdf)

Some state programs separately require completion/final-delivery reporting through CalSMART and treatment of savings. Resolve each program's reporting events rather than substituting federal FROE for them. [LAPG 25](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapg/g25.pdf)

## 6. Audit, privacy and records design

For federal awards, current 2 CFR §200.302 calls for award identification, complete financial disclosure, source-supported records of authorizations/obligations/balances/expenditures, budget comparison and written allowability procedures. Design the export so an auditor can navigate from a reported figure to those sources. OpenPlan should complement the agency's accounting system, with reconciled imports/exports and a named system of record, rather than claim to replace statutory accounting from a few invoice statuses. [Current §200.302](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-D/section-200.302)

Maintain a project file index with versions, custody, signatories, review state, permitted audience, amendments and missing-record notices. Caltrans' Construction Manual project-record guidance offers a reusable filing/record model where applicable; off-SHS agencies need their adopted index rather than an assertion that every Caltrans form is universally mandatory. [Construction Manual §5-1](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/construction/construction-manual/section-5-1-project-records-and-reports)

LAPM §16.3.2 starts its described three-year federal project retention period at Caltrans' submission of the final voucher to FHWA—not at the local “complete” button. Store the actual trigger evidence, and have the records officer resolve other agreement/state/agency requirements and litigation/audit holds. Do not automate deletion from this research. Payroll, employee interviews, bank/remittance records, bids and claims can require narrower access than a general project collaborator; public exports need deliberate redaction. [LAPM 16](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch16.pdf)

LAPM Chapter 20 expressly examines consultant procurement, grant/contract management, direct/indirect costs and invoice/labor records, with corrective-action processes. Its Single Audit threshold is stated across the agency's total federal expenditures, not separately per bridge. Add audit requests, evidence responses, findings, disputed amounts, corrective actions and verified resolution; do not manufacture a project-level “no audit required” verdict from a small award. [LAPM 20](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/local-assistance/documents/lapm/ch20.pdf)

Test restore/export of a representative multi-year construction file, including private attachments and local worker artifacts. A backup or evidence bundle containing only metadata is insufficient. Hashes establish byte identity, not professional correctness or completeness; both require review.
