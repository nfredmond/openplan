# Washington and Minnesota local transportation manuals reconnaissance

Reviewed September 4, 2026, Pacific. Active OpenPlan HEAD was `01f0fd0390b578de282802deb53b33cf98208047`, initially clean. This was public-source research only. No checkout edits, tests, application/browser/account activity or external submission occurred.

## Recommendation

Examine both states early. Washington tests whether OpenPlan can represent changing delegated authority and state-only planning work. Minnesota tests formula allocations, designated road systems, sponsoring agencies and financial transfers that never become the recipient's cash. These are useful counterexamples to a California-shaped implementation. They belong within existing nationwide coverage, authority, delivery, finance and program milestones, not separate state applications.

This is reconnaissance, not full-manual reading or a certification that either state is supported. Broader state coverage should continue through source inventories, selected contrasting workflows and practitioner acceptance before full operational claims.

## Washington differences that matter

**Delegation is conditional and can change.** [LAG Chapter 13](https://wsdot.wa.gov/publications/manuals/fulltext/M36-63/Lag13.pdf), §§13.1–13.6, printed pp. 13-1 through 13-4, describes Certification Acceptance. Qualification includes organization/staff capability, interview, trial/mentoring and project-management review. Certification normally continues unless modified or rescinded; reviews, staffing changes or other findings can reduce project/phase authority or create probation. Relevant personnel changes require notice within 30 calendar days. A non-certified agency may use region-led administration or an approved agreement with another certified agency.

The chapter distinguishes a Washington professional engineer used in administration from the full-time agency employee in responsible charge when consultants are used. Do not merge these into one role or infer that a contracted engineer automatically satisfies federal responsible charge. Certification also leaves approvals outside the agency, including PS&E before advertisement, project final acceptance and ROW certification. Review newer bulletins before implementing detailed retained-approval lists. An OpenPlan owner/admin role cannot confer any of this authority.

**State-only planning has its own path.** [Chapter 15](https://wsdot.wa.gov/publications/manuals/fulltext/M36-63/Lag15.pdf), §§15.1–15.3, pp. 15-1 through 15-3, describes project risk assessment, state-funded planning/TDM packages and state environmental requirements. Regionally significant state/local-only projects can still require programming; the MPO/RTPO determines significance. Anticipated federal funding changes environmental/ROW expectations. A funding-source label alone is insufficient to derive every prerequisite.

**Billing has local rules beyond generic reimbursement.** [Chapter 23](https://wsdot.wa.gov/publications/manuals/fulltext/M36-63/Lag23.pdf), §§23.1–23.4, pp. 23-1 through 23-3, separates agreement execution and written authorization. It requires monthly, sequential progress bills and explanation for overlapping/gapped periods. Initial support includes signatory authority. Zero-dollar bills are processed only as final; negative claims require a credit explanation and repayment. The final summary reports all funding sources. These distinctions need versioned packet and reconciliation rules, not renamed Caltrans exhibits or a universal zero-activity invoice.

## Minnesota differences that matter

**State-aid allocation depends on a designated network and needs.** State Aid Manual Chapters 1–3, pp. 5–18, describes CSAH/MSAS systems, screening boards, mileage certification, formal designation/revocation, construction/maintenance accounts and annual apportionment. Its Needs estimates support distribution and explicitly are not instructions for actual design/construction. Route revocation can cause a remaining-life adjustment against subsequent construction funding. Model legal network status, engineering quantities, allocation basis and project design estimate separately. Obtain current statutory/formula and screening-board sources before calculating production allocations; this reconnaissance does not validate every historical percentage in the manual. [Manual](https://edocs-public.dot.state.mn.us/edocs_public/DMResultSet/download?docId=25636424), Chapters 2–3.

**Sponsorship is a fiscal and delivery responsibility.** Manual §5.3.X, pp. 111–114, describes sponsors assisting less-experienced recipients, fronting funds and potentially facing recovery. A consultant designated city engineer cannot be responsible charge for its own firm's project. [July 2026 DCP checklist](https://edocs-public.dot.state.mn.us/edocs_public/DMResultSet/download?docId=36671935), pp. 1–3, makes qualification project-specific, with DCP 01 request, staffing review, agency agreement and financial-capacity steps. Store applicant, recipient, sponsor, owner, administrator and payer as separate relationships with actual agreements. A generic collaborator invitation does not establish sponsorship.

**Construction finance has more than one money path.** County accounting manual Chapter 6, printed pp. 99–101, distinguishes county-administered DCP from MnDOT-administered traditional contracts. In the latter, matching state funds transfer between state accounts rather than reaching county cash; local funding can be billed before award. State-aid advances and their later reimbursement are distinct from federal reimbursement. Page 99 describes payment before requesting reimbursement; p. 100 also permits incurred-expense requests under an approved process assuring prompt contractor payment after reimbursement. Preserve that condition and require finance confirmation; do not universally inherit California's paid-cost gate. [Accounting manual](https://edocs-public.dot.state.mn.us/edocs_public/DMResultSet/download?docId=2961608).

The DCP payment guide, pp. 5–9, distinguishes voucher number, cumulative certified amounts by category, retainage and prorated/nonprorated deductions. Its [final-packet instructions](https://www.dot.state.mn.us/safinance/dcp-final-docs.html) require matching original bid categories, signed final voucher, change support and specific not-applicable material documentation. A state-aid payment form may be required because work is eligible even when state-aid payment is not requested. Export is not receipt, and the SAAS guide's legacy ActiveX/password instructions were not verified operationally.

## Aerial follow-up: Washington funding restrictions

[LPB-2026-02 R2](https://wsdot.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/wsdot-lpb-2026-02-r2-federal-restrictions-on-foreign-drones.pdf), March 10, 2026, pp. 1–2, states an effective date of December 22, 2025. It prohibits covered-UAS procurement, operation, maintenance and data processing on projects with Title 23 funds, even if equipment uses other funding or its costs are unbilled. It covers consultants/contractors across planning through maintenance and requires contract restrictions. Blue List equipment qualifies by default; other equipment needs manufacturer/component compliance certifications and cybersecurity assessment. This is Washington's stated implementation, not a universal DJI prohibition. OpenPlan should retain project funding, equipment/component provenance, compliance evidence and processing eligibility before mission or imagery workflows proceed. USB export capability alone establishes none of these. Read both pages fully; underlying OMB/FASC criteria, exceptions and subsequent contract-provision revisions were not independently resolved here.
