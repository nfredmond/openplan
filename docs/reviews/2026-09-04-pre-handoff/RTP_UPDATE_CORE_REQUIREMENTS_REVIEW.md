## Finding and scope

OpenPlan already has a substantial RTP foundation: prior-document intake and transcription review, chapter editing and assisted drafting, project portfolios, financial and performance records, public engagement, and HTML/PDF exports. **It has not established the complete planner job of importing the previous adopted RTP, deciding what to retain or change, updating every applicable element, reconciling the resulting plan, and preserving its adoption and subsequent amendments.** Existing source supports partial capability, not complete outcome acceptance.

Nathaniel's requirement restores the intended core scope. Financial tables, policies, action programs and draft narrative are not optional enhancements. Nor are they the entire scope: the update must account for every applicable section, appendix, map, table, consultation record and implementation commitment. California is the first deep implementation, within the unchanged v1 ambition covering all 50 states and DC and all core planning work. Territorial scope remains an unresolved product question; this report changes none of it.

Extend the present RTP workroom, document library, project/program records and shared evidence infrastructure. A new parallel plan-authoring module would fragment the same planner's work. The review distinguishes **documented behavior**, **observed source**, **inferred workflow gaps**, and **proposed acceptance**. Nothing below claims that I observed a person complete the interface.

## Authority and applicability checked against current primary sources

The [CTC transportation-planning index](https://catc.ca.gov/programs/transportation-planning) and [Caltrans guidelines page](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/transportation-planning/division-of-transportation-planning/regional-and-community-planning/rtp-guidelines-update) currently identify the separate MPO and RTPA guidelines adopted January 26, 2024. The indices establish the current published edition; the guidelines contain both requirements and recommended practice. Do not turn every recommendation into a universal statutory gate.

California Government Code §65080 requires an internally consistent RTP with policy, action and financial elements. Policy objectives must fit financial estimates; implementation responsibilities belong in the action element; the financial element addresses realistic revenues and allocation. The statute assigns SCS preparation to MPOs and conditionally requires an APS. It also addresses modal breadth, updates and public hearing requirements. Agency-specific provisions and exceptions exist. These distinctions must survive imports and template selection. [Current §65080](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=65080.)

Federal metropolitan-plan rules require at least a 20-year horizon, with four-year updates in nonattainment/maintenance areas and five-year updates in attainment areas. Contents include current/projected demand, facilities, performance, strategies, mitigation and a financial plan. Cost and revenue estimates use year-of-expenditure assumptions; aggregate outer-year cost bands are permitted beyond the first ten years. Financial evidence must address implementation and system operations/maintenance. A numeric surplus alone cannot establish this. [23 CFR 450.324](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-23/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-450/subpart-C/section-450.324)

MPO participation requirements include accessible information, consultation, consideration and response to input, and additional comment opportunity when a final plan introduces significant changes and new material issues. Significant comments require a disposition record in the final plan. The 45-day minimum in §450.316(a)(3) concerns the **participation plan**, and must not become a universal draft-RTP deadline. [23 CFR 450.316](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-23/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-450/subpart-C/section-450.316)

A non-MPO California RTPA is not automatically an MPO for every federal obligation. The RTPA guidelines address nonmetropolitan applicability and distinguish requirements from recommendations, including analysis groupings. Statewide/nonmetropolitan rules have their own applicability. Neither a California mailing address nor a workspace's state identifies the agency's full legal role. [RTPA guidelines, §§1–3 and Appendix A](https://catc.ca.gov/-/media/ctc-media/documents/programs/transportation-planning/2024-rtp-guidelines/adopted-2024-rtp-guidelines-for-rtpas.pdf), [23 CFR 450.202](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-23/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-450/subpart-B/section-450.202)

The MPO guidelines separately cover SCS/APS, analysis, consultation, environment, modal planning, finances and performance. Their financial discussion includes revenue evidence, O&M, constrained/illustrative lists and shortfall strategies. Their chapter organization is useful for completeness review, but need not dictate an agency's publication outline. [MPO guidelines, chapters 3–7, especially §§6.1–6.6](https://catc.ca.gov/-/media/ctc-media/documents/programs/transportation-planning/adopted-2024-rtp-guidelines-for-mpos.pdf)

The federal planning factors also cover economic vitality, safety/security, mobility/accessibility, environment and land-use consistency, connectivity, operations, preservation, resilience/stormwater and tourism. These broaden the product benchmark beyond a highway project list. [23 CFR 450.306](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-23/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-450/subpart-C/section-450.306)

An actual publication provides a useful independent completeness check: BCAG's official 2024 RTP/SCS page publishes clean and tracked-change versions, a signed adoption resolution, fourteen appendices, environmental findings and a mitigation monitoring program, and a later CARB approval record. This is documented agency practice, not an OpenPlan test fixture or a universal required file list. [BCAG adopted-plan publication](https://bcag.specialdistrict.org/2024-rtp-scs-update)

**Source limitations:** I sampled the guidelines' contents, applicability, financial, consultation and checklist passages, not every page of the 308-page MPO and 207-page RTPA documents. Current eCFR pages showed September 2026 currency, but access to §450.216 redirected to an access challenge. Older FHWA financial-guidance URLs failed to open. The available [FHWA planning-process handbook](https://highways.fhwa.dot.gov/media/48411), pp.23–25, supports the distinction between financial planning and programming, but contains older program references and is not used as a current-law authority. Agency-specific conformity, CEQA, civil-rights, SCS determinations and later legislative changes need their own dated applicability review before legal-completeness claims. I did not audit all states' RTP equivalents.

### The financial element is useful arithmetic, not complete financial assurance

Existing [financial types and calculation](/home/nathaniel/code/openplan/openplan/src/lib/rtp/fiscal-constraint.ts:39) represent agency-defined periods, revenues, O&M, other costs, constrained project costs, basis years, inflation, period balances and unresolved projects. Missing project prices, unassigned periods and uncovered horizon years prevent a determination. The [schema](/home/nathaniel/code/openplan/openplan/supabase/migrations/20260805000003_rtp_financial_element.sql:157) and existing editors should be extended, not replaced.

Present limits are precise:

- The input is cycle-wide, without scenario or alternative identity (`fiscal-constraint.ts:74–87`). The RTP page does read linked projects' scenario-comparison snapshots (`page.tsx:584–597`), so scenario evidence is not wholly absent. That summary is not a preferred-alternative financial ledger.
- The calculator has no evidence field establishing that a revenue is reasonably available, no missing-O&M completeness state, and no inventory of expected-but-unpriced ledger rows. Ledger amounts are required; a planner who cannot price a line omits it under the schema's present convention. Missing O&M can therefore contribute zero while the aggregate verdict says constrained. The sentence [describing the result](/home/nathaniel/code/openplan/openplan/src/lib/rtp/fiscal-constraint.ts:515) calls recorded revenue “reasonably available”; arithmetic alone has not established that premise.
- Period findings are computed separately (`:426–456`), but the whole-cycle verdict uses summary blockers and total balance (`:463–476`), without requiring each period to be constrained. A future-period surplus can offset an earlier deficit in that headline; no carryover/borrowing justification is an input. This is source-derived behavior, not a tested legal violation. RTP and TIP/STIP financial timing rules differ; do not fix this by importing a universal annual TIP rule into every RTP.
- Dollar-basis disclosure already distinguishes constant from expenditure-year dollars. That correction does not establish legal adequacy, correct escalation assumptions, restricted-fund eligibility, matching requirements, overlapping sources, or approved revenue forecasts.
- The generated [financial table](/home/nathaniel/code/openplan/openplan/src/lib/rtp/export.ts:700) prints revenue, projects, O&M and balance but no `other_cost` column, although other costs enter the balance. It also does not print the full revenue-source ledger in that section. A reviewer cannot reconstruct all arithmetic from that summary table alone.

## Sequencing and a complete prior-plan-update acceptance

These are implementation and evaluation proposals, not work performed. Root owns the active roadmap; this report does not reorder it.

1. **Establish the plan and its inherited record.** Select an actual adopted rural RTPA plan and an actual MPO RTP/SCS, with permission to use their public materials. Preserve source files, amendments and appendices; set plan-owned authority and responsible agencies. Implement the missing text-extraction control using existing targets. Keep unprocessed pages, unsupported formats and ambiguous records visible. A source manifest must account for all old contents through mapped, retained-as-attachment, superseded, irrelevant-with-reason or unresolved dispositions. Do not promise complete import because the model returned candidates.

2. **Make complete human authoring possible.** Extend the existing chapters into an agency-controlled document structure and stable policy/action records. The planner must revise inherited text, create new policies, retire obsolete actions, incorporate staff/partner changes and attach source material without leaving the system for hidden database work. Preserve the distinction between quotation, machine draft and human-approved agency language. Provide usable manual paths and a local/free extraction option consistent with v1's operating boundary.

3. **Complete fiscal and alternative consistency.** Reuse the financial calculator and editors; add explicit expected costs, source availability evidence, applicable financing assumptions and alternative identity. Every table must reconcile, including other costs. Changing one project's scope or inflation assumption must identify affected totals, narratives and alternatives. Test lawful funding timing with agency-supported assumptions; do not invent a universal fiscal threshold or classify unsupported revenues as available.

4. **Complete the evidence and participation joins.** Reuse GIS, Safety, Land Use Plans, Scenarios, Programs, Engagement, Documents and Reports. The plan's selected evidence must carry its geography, year, method and use limitation through every chapter and export. Environmental and scientific adequacy are independent acceptance boundaries. Until those are met, a complete authoring workflow may manage external agency-approved analyses, but must label OpenPlan's own unsupported model uses and cannot claim v1 scientific readiness.

5. **Preserve adoption and amendments.** Human review creates the exact adopted revision and records its resolutions, approvals, remaining work and submission history. Later edits create a traceable draft/amendment rather than silently changing that revision. A public comment links to the draft actually reviewed, and a significant revision triggers a human applicability decision about further review. Export the complete plan, detailed editable tables, GIS layers and evidence manifest, including appendix relationships and source limitations.

**Acceptance is one continuous planner job, not five disconnected demos.** From the normal signed-in entry point, a planner starts with a real previous adopted plan and completes a successor draft containing all applicable work above. A second planner checks it against the source and revises it. A financial reviewer independently reconstructs the totals. A public reviewer reads and comments on the identified draft. Authorized staff resolve or explicitly retain outstanding issues, produce a board package, record a simulated adoption in a test environment, make an amendment and retrieve the unchanged prior revision. Finally, a new staff member starts the following update from the preserved record. No engineer, SQL console, fabricated source data or unrecorded manual repair may bridge a step.

Evidence must include identified checkout/build, desktop and 390px journeys, keyboard operation, accessible reading order and table semantics, console review, persistence across reload/logout, role isolation, editable export reconciliation and independently inspected PDF pagination/legibility. Browser evidence is needed for reachability; source and unit tests cannot substitute. Human participants should include a rural RTPA planner, MPO planner, finance/programming reviewer and public reviewer, with assistance needs recorded rather than hidden.

**Falsification cases to register before accepting the workflow:** a relevant policy outside the first selected passages; a scanned multi-page financial table; a repeated project name with distinct phases; an old amendment contradicting the base document; missing O&M; a revenue without availability evidence; an early deficit offset only by future revenue; a debt-service line affecting balance; a changed preferred alternative; an unvalidated model claim; an unmoderated/private comment; a response to a superseded draft; a missing appendix; an adopted revision edited afterward; and a download whose rows no longer reconcile with the screen. Each must produce the intended visible limitation, refusal or review task. Use surviving no-op and targeted mutations when implementing guards, but do not describe this proposed test set as executed.
