# Engagement mapping priority: a defensible path beyond Social Pinpoint

Research dated 2026-09-04. Nathaniel's priority is engagement mapping better than socialpinpoint.com. The recommendation is to make an accessible, durable **contribution-to-decision journey** OpenPlan's flagship engagement outcome: a resident understands the proposal, identifies a place, contributes without losing work, receives a usable receipt, and later sees the agency's response and the resulting plan/project action. An agency can moderate, analyze, explain and transfer the complete record without rebuilding it elsewhere.

OpenPlan cannot yet substantiate a superiority claim. Social Pinpoint documents a substantial product, and OpenPlan has consequential gaps in basic public evidence and continuity. Competing on attractive pins, a language selector, or a list beside a map would set the bar too low. The attainable advantage is the quality of the entire planning job, including public and staff accessibility, honest evidence, controlled publication, independent reuse and free agency ownership.

### Accessibility: use the newer evidence and read its exclusions

The currently linked **30 March 2026** report covers public-facing features and excludes dashboard, reporting, administration and user multimedia. It records partial support for keyboard operation in the ranking tool and for some dialog/expand-collapse semantics; it also notes survey-submission announcements and other exceptions. It is not a blanket certification of every configured site. [March 2026 conformance report](https://www.openpoint.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Social-Point-VPAT-March-2026.pdf).

The older **18 December 2025** report additionally named map-post comments in its keyboard exception. That wording is absent from the March report; current map help also documents the accessible feed. Do not present the old map-comment exception as a demonstrated current defect. Nor does its removal prove every interaction is now accessible. [December 2025 report](https://www.socialpinpoint.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Social-Pinpoint-VPAT-December-2025.pdf).

A useful OpenPlan advantage would be independently evaluated complete public **and operator** processes, with published versioned exceptions and an accessible alternative when a map interaction is unsuitable. That is a proposed standard, not a claim that OpenPlan currently meets it.

## Present gaps that obstruct a superior experience

1. **The public record is silently bounded to the newest 200 approved items.** `lib/engagement/public-portal-data.ts:1013-1019` orders newest first and applies `.limit(200)`; its returned bundle at `1185-1206` has no continuation or total/partial-state metadata. The sampled feed renders that array. Once more than 200 approved records exist, older contributions are outside this public view. Moreover, `approved-item-grouping.ts:59-76` drops a reply if its parent is absent from the loaded set; a capped-out parent can therefore hide an otherwise approved recent reply. This is a source-established completeness defect, not a reproduced live campaign. Implement proper paging/querying with thread integrity and explicit totals rather than merely raising the cap or loading every marker at once.

2. **The API's usable receipt is discarded before the resident sees it.** The submit endpoint returns `submissionId` and `reviewStatus`; `lib/engagement/submit-portal-input.ts:55-58,126-132` reduces success to `{ok:true}`, and the form at `448-450,479-495` displays a generic received state. There is no receipt identifier in that path, and the success block itself has no explicit live-status semantics. Preserve a stable receipt and status explanation, with appropriate anonymous access protection, before promising later status tracking. Screen-reader announcement needs a real test; source inspection alone cannot settle it.

3. **Opening context can abandon a comment draft.** Comment text and geometry live in component state (`portal-submission-form.tsx:212-250`, `public-map-shell.tsx:243-249`), while the map's context door is a normal anchor at `public-map-shell.tsx:479-495`. No comment-draft persistence or navigation guard was found in the sampled form/helper. Survey drafts do not rescue this separate flow. Test “start input, read the proposal, return” and refresh/interruption explicitly. Saving sensitive drafts requires deliberate device/expiry/clear controls, especially on a shared library computer.

4. **Anti-abuse safeguards can exclude a public meeting.** `lib/engagement/public-submit.ts:4-6,48-66,130-169` keys the connection fingerprint to IP and allows three recent submissions in ten minutes; duplicate text is checked across the loaded campaign records, not just a verified person. The route returns 429 or 409 at `347-360`. Shared Wi-Fi, a kiosk, carrier NAT, and people independently entering the same short statement are plausible legitimate collision cases. This is a source-based risk, not a demonstrated production denial. Test them before claiming inclusive access. Keep bot resistance, durable retries and human review without treating one IP or identical wording as one person.

5. **Language breadth exceeds translated interface breadth.** `portal-i18n/messages.ts:847-848` registers Spanish beside the English source; other offered locales can contain English fallback interface text. The disclosure is honest but does not make the interface usable in those languages. Prioritize fully reviewed campaign journeys and errors in actual community languages, retaining original participant text and translation provenance. Do not count a language-menu entry as language access.

6. **Publication needs field-level privacy and useful public derivatives.** Public approved records include names and exact geometry (`public-portal-data.ts:1185-1199`). Photo upload writes validated original bytes at `api/engage/[shareToken]/photo-upload/route.ts:121-123`; no metadata-stripping step appears in that route, and approved-photo signed URLs are issued at `public-portal-data.ts:1152-1166`. A photo may carry location metadata distinct from its mapped issue. No private data disclosure was demonstrated. Before publication, preview exactly which name, image, geometry, text and metadata will leave the agency; create a reviewed redacted/approximate derivative when needed while retaining lawful originals and audit history. “Anonymous” must not conceal retained connection data or identify a location as someone's home.

7. **A response narrative is not yet proof of a resolved decision.** Close-loop entries store theme text and source IDs, but the sampled public component presents the narrative rather than an inspectable link to each source, exact adopted version and implementation owner/status. `loadCloseLoopEntries` at `close-loop.ts:69-76` also discards its query error on the operator path, unlike the public loader. A failed read could look like a missing response and prompt duplicate authoring. Extend the existing source references, project/plan records and approvals; preserve “considered but not adopted,” unanswered and unresolved states.

8. **OpenPlan has no demonstrated whole-process accessibility or operational advantage.** `portal-accessibility-notice.tsx:47-60` offers configured alternatives and explicitly avoids claiming conformance. A contact block, keyboard handler or responsive class is not an audit. Public map rendering currently depends on the configured Mapbox path (`public-map-stage.tsx:811-824`); a key-free text fallback is useful, but it is not proof of a complete free mapping service. Agency moderation staffing, backups, public exposure, language review and recovery must work without Nathaniel. Social Pinpoint's documented staffed operation is a real benchmark, not something software alone replaces.

## Outcome benchmark and superior-experience requirements

These are proposed acceptance tasks, to be fixed before comparative testing. They are not claims that the competitor fails them. Use the same legitimate agency question, proposal, available source layers and public content in both products where possible. Record configuration and entitlement differences rather than quietly giving one side a richer setup.

| Outcome | Required OpenPlan behavior | Evidence that rejects completion |
|---|---|---|
| Understand the invitation | From a flyer/QR/deep link, explain the decision, area, participation period, what can change, who decides and how input will be used. Plain-language context stays available beside the task. | Participant mistakes consultation for a binding vote, cannot find the affected area, or loses a draft when opening context. |
| Identify the place | Locate an issue by map, named place, coordinate or description; optionally describe a route/area; review the location's meaning and uncertainty. Provide an equivalent nonvisual route even when the map loads. | A user must drag precisely, the inferred address changes their meaning, an addressless/tribal/rural place cannot be expressed, or a location gets silently snapped elsewhere. |
| Contribute on a phone | Complete input at 390px, with keyboard open, orientation changes, zoom and a weak connection. Keep the active control visible, allow correction, preserve work and clearly acknowledge receipt. | Covered submit control, accidental vertex, double submission, vanished text/photo, stale success after failure, or missing durable receipt. |
| Participate without a pointer or sight | Complete map/list discovery, geometry or equivalent location, form, ranking, review, send and receipt using keyboard and representative assistive technology. Preserve focus/scroll context. | Trap, unlabeled action, hidden focus, drag-only step, unread status or an alternative route that omits meaningful information. |
| Read and respond at scale | Search/filter all approved public contributions with stable links, comprehensible ordering, map/list correspondence and complete threads. Distinguish votes, comments and people. | An older relevant item or parent disappears at the 200-row boundary; list/map/export disagree; counts imply full or representative coverage they do not possess. |
| Know what becomes public | Preview the public name/text/photo/location, consent separately to optional contact/demographics, understand moderation and withdrawal/correction channels. | Sensitive original or image metadata leaks into the public derivative; “private” export contains public assumptions; a generic privacy page contradicts the actual task. |
| Moderate fairly and sustainably | Staff review flagged/pending items, see reasons and context, protect sensitive information, undo mistakes, keep an audit and communicate disposition where authorized. Human decisions remain accountable. | AI silently rejects dissent, translated meaning is lost, shared-event participants are blocked, or moderation queue/error/notification states are concealed. |
| Include meeting and assisted input | Bring permitted paper/email/meeting records into the same review process with source and transcription attribution, separate duplicates, and no automatic approval. | Imported participants vanish from summaries, comments are mislabeled as staff opinion, repeated uploads inflate influence, or a volunteer must impersonate the resident. |
| Answer the public | Trace a receipt/contribution through an approved agency response to the exact decision or unresolved issue, then to a named action where adopted. Subscribers receive only promised, authorized updates. | A general “we heard you” paragraph cannot be traced, a response claims an action never approved, later amendment severs the record, or an unanswered issue is made to look resolved. |
| Produce reusable agency evidence | Export complete selected comments, original/translated text, geometry, status, source, response links and permitted attachments with a readable manifest; create a safe public derivative. A second analyst opens it in independent tools. | Pagination/filters silently change totals, attachments lose IDs, geometry/CRS is ambiguous, formulas execute, private moderation/demographic details enter a public handoff, or recipient must reconstruct relationships. |
| Understand whose input is missing | Separate respondents, submissions, support actions, referral channels and missing demographic data; compare only defensible populations and disclose uncertainty. | Issue-pin location is treated as residence, repeated contributors count as distinct residents, ecological traits become individual identities, or a participation count becomes a representative mandate. |
| Operate after staff turnover | Another operator opens/closes a consultation, hands over moderation, handles a failed notification, restores records and publishes an approved response on a supported free deployment. | Founder intervention, undisclosed paid entitlement, lost originals, unreviewed publication or untested restore. |

Use **WCAG 2.2 AA** as the engineering/evaluation target for full pages and complete processes, including staff tools. Relevant criteria include keyboard access, focus, labels/errors, status announcements, reflow, language, target size and alternatives to dragging. Its target-size minimum is 24 CSS pixels with specified exceptions; a larger touch target can be an OpenPlan design preference, not a misquoted legal requirement. The normal content surrounding a map still needs reflow even where the map itself requires two-dimensional presentation. This report does not determine any agency's legal compliance deadline. [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

## Recommended implementation order within this priority

1. **Make the current public record reliable.** Repair public pagination/thread completeness, return usable receipts, preserve comment drafts through context/retry, and test shared-connection participation. Keep the current retained flagged-honeypot correction and existing source/approval limits. Complete real desktop/390px/keyboard/error journeys before adding more tools.
2. **Deliver one exceptionally clear public mapping experience.** Reuse the shared form, map stage, geometry, context-layer and translation systems. Bring context, list/search, contribution and response into a coherent navigation flow; avoid another detached form. Make nonvisual location and map/list parity first-class. Retain source geometry and let residents say what is uncertain.
3. **Complete staff review and privacy.** Join moderation, bilingual review, field-level publication preview, safe image derivatives, operational queue ownership and accessible staff controls. Keep permission decisions and final public content human-owned. Preserve legitimate opposing views and the distinction between moderation, duplicate handling and analysis.
4. **Prove the decision and recipient handoff.** Connect source-item references to current project/plan decisions and implementation actions, using existing evidence bundles, Reports and My Work. Export the actual record and permitted attachments to a second analyst. Preserve unresolved/disagreed cases instead of forcing every contribution into a success story.
5. **Evaluate comparative superiority and publish the limits.** First run internal and independent OpenPlan tasks; then, if legitimate competitor access is authorized, conduct matched comparative evaluation. Only claim superiority for outcomes actually demonstrated. Continue source/language/archetype expansion without shrinking the nationwide planning ambition.

This is an extension of Engagement and existing planning/GIS/evidence modules. It is not a recommendation for a new engagement product, a parallel review engine, or a copy of the competitor's visual design. Free/open operation can differentiate OpenPlan, but only after useful mapping, restoration and agency administration work on that route. Do not promise free round-the-clock human moderation or automatic expert translation.

## Human evaluation and falsification

Recruitment/outreach belongs to Nathaniel. Prepare tasks, consent, accessibility accommodations and approved test content first; send no unsolicited contact. Include residents with differing map literacy and languages, keyboard/screen-reader users, mobile/low-bandwidth users, participants from a shared public event, agency moderators and GIS/records recipients. Use permitted real planning questions and safe synthetic edge cases; label synthetic material unmistakably.

For a later comparative study, counterbalance product order and equivalent tasks, give equivalent agency configuration, and record product/version/date. Separate public tasks, staff tasks and cross-product transfer. Avoid leading instructions that teach OpenPlan's architecture or reveal a desired winner. Record unassisted completion, correctness of the location and submitted record, recovery, privacy/decision comprehension, time/effort, needed assistance and recipient reuse. Retain failures and reasons, including people for whom neither product works. A rating that someone “liked it” is supporting feedback, not outcome proof.

Set the practical success criteria, required contexts and any quantitative comparison rule before seeing results; do not invent a sample size or superiority percentage in this report. Analyze by task and participant context so an average cannot conceal a blocked assistive-technology or language group. No improvement in speed offsets a privacy exposure or lost contribution. Where competitor access or a comparable task is unavailable, report **not compared**. Documentary benchmarking supports a development target, not a current “better than Social Pinpoint” marketing claim.

Independent engineering verification should inspect stored records and exported bytes; exercise failed submissions, lost responses, retries, revoked publication, missing parents, source-layer replacement and unknown data; and show that changed consequential guards can fail while harmless mutations survive. Actual browser identity, console, desktop/390px and assistive-technology evidence remain required for visible behavior. A live screenshot cannot establish complete export data, and a green source fixture cannot establish a resident's understanding.
