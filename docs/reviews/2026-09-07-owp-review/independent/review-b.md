## Actionable findings

### B1. High: public-copy review consent survives a change of content

`openplan/src/components/programs/work-program/workflow.tsx:17` stores `publicReviewed` independently of revision and event history. The revision selector at line 65 changes only `selected`. Line 86 clears the checkbox only when the audience changes. Line 88 passes its value to a newly keyed exporter for another revision or sequence.

An administrator can check that revision A and its history were reviewed for disclosure, select revision B, and prepare B's public copy without renewed consent. A new saved revision or history event has the same problem. The SQL packet guard requires the boolean, so the stale browser boolean crosses the actual disclosure gate. Copies remain private in Documents until someone distributes them, but the approved public-copy claim is already wrong.

Bind the attestation to the exact revision ID, hash, history sequence and audience. Clear it whenever that identity changes. Verify revision switch, new save and intervening event transitions at desktop and 390px. A harmless text change must preserve the check and removing the identity reset must fail it. This is a source-established counterexample; browser reproduction remains required.

### B2. High: an amendment blocks later authority evidence for the effective baseline

`openplan/supabase/migrations/20260909000001_work_program_review.sql:76` requires `external_acceptance` and `spending_authorization` to target the latest revision. Lines 108-109 separately require those events to target the effective adopted revision. Starting an amendment at lines 134-135 creates a new preparation revision while retaining the old adopted baseline.

After that normal transition, neither revision satisfies both conditions. A later-arriving external approval or spending authorization for the still-effective adopted program is refused until the amendment is itself adopted. The product explicitly says current edits do not change the effective baseline, so the restriction contradicts its intended administration model.

Exempt these two authority-evidence kinds from the latest-only condition while retaining exact effective-revision, hash, membership, authority, date, scope and document checks. Reproduce adopt revision A, start amendment B, then record a real-source-bounded acceptance for A. B must remain unapproved. Also reject evidence targeting an unrelated historical revision or B. The paired conditions establish the refusal in source; I did not execute the database case.

## The ten protocol questions

1. **What makes this the ultimate free planning operating system?** A planner can carry an actual case from inherited source material through analysis, public input, responsible decisions, funded delivery and a successor's usable archive without re-entering facts across modules. Every consequential figure and authority has an owner, version and source. The product works without an agent, without Nathaniel administering each installation, and without a required paid service. Optional agents do substantial grounded work under the same permissions and approvals.

2. **Can every US planner do their core work here?** No. The current capability matrix describes foundations and unassessed complete jobs. Missing or shallow outcomes include full OWP reporting and closeout, planning contract drawdown and forecasts, complete prior-RTP updates, local-tax/grant recipient administration, procurement from both sides, environmental commitments, development conditions and interdepartmental review, statutory hearing records, tribal governance, full public records and independent recovery. Aerial planning through usable quality-reviewed products, nationwide multimodal practice and platform-wide agent work remain explicit obligations. No module count settles these jobs.

3. **Does every state work substantively?** No such evidence exists in the current matrix. California is partial; the other states and DC remain unassessed against the complete contract. All fifty states and DC stay required. Alaska, Hawaii, rural/border/island settings, tribal and overlapping authorities cannot inherit proof from one California case. Territories retain explicit coverage and worldwide extension remains architectural scope. Source availability, a resolved place and honest degradation are useful but do not satisfy substantive practice.

4. **Is California the gold standard?** Not yet. The retained public-source EDCTC preparation case supplies bounded M2d.1 engineering evidence. It does not prove an RTPA, MPO, county, city, tribal organization and state agency can each complete the relevant agency cycle. The matrix still names land-use authority and plan-kind limitations, incomplete capital/local-assistance administration, and broader outcome gaps. Preserve California's deepest proof obligation across metropolitan, rural, mountain, coastal, border and tribal settings.

5. **Are both travel models scientifically validated for every published use nationwide?** No. Current authorities preserve separate AequilibraE and ActivitySim outputs and inconclusive development findings. This review generated no new scientific evidence. The historical one-county 43.3% selection metric, model agreement and LODES home-to-work loading cannot establish nationwide all-purpose accuracy. Keep consumed holdouts sealed, no averaging, no scalar-fitting or default changes to improve an exposed score, and no national aggregate that hides a failing state or required stratum.

6. **What simple overlooked idea has the largest effect?** Make every case answer, in one coherent view, "what version governs, what is pending, who acts next, and where is the evidence?" The existing work-program workflow is a useful starting point. B2 and the returned-work gap show why an event history alone does not answer the operational question. Extend existing My Work and domain records to preserve the obligation after a return, amendment or late-arriving approval. This should improve OWP, capital, contracts, procurement and RTP workflows without another task system.

7. **Which old rule or decision is wrong?** Requiring every new authority record to reference the latest editable version is wrong when the governing baseline remains older. B2 is the concrete counterexample. Treating a generated packet as accepted delivery, M2d.1 preparation as full OWP, a refreshed review date as a reassessment, or v0.44 package metadata as a release is also wrong. I found no basis to reverse the full v1 contract, replace the single roadmap queue or restart the established preparation workflow.

A current source and live test showing consent already bound to exact packet identity would refute B1; the cited state flow currently does not. Independently opening the delivered artifacts, explaining authority and retrieving every needed permitted original would falsify the recipient gap.
