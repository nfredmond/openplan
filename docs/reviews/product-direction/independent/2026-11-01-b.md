## 1. Bottom line

OpenPlan has a large, careful foundation and a wide set of record types. A planner in California, or in Oregon on the tested paths, can finish several bounded jobs. Engagement and the work program (OWP) are the deepest. Elsewhere the product is far less complete than its documents suggest. The system that is supposed to measure progress toward v1 has stopped moving:

- **Zero of 99 capability cells are proven.** The registry was last reconciled September 6. Its review date of October 5 has passed (`US_PLANNING_CAPABILITY_REGISTRY.json`: 37 partial, 62 not-assessed).
- **Only 3 of 56 jurisdictions are in the readiness registry** (California, Oregon and Puerto Rico), and its review date of September 28 has passed (`registry.v1.json`).
- **The twelve first-week journeys have not had a full run since September 6** (v0.44, nine passes and three partials). The roadmap's October 15 "next three" list still names that rerun as item 2.
- **The previous direction review's chosen next outcome is unfinished.** It picked a complete engagement case, contribution to decision. The roadmap's M9b next action still reads "Trace one contribution from source to theme, response and decision."

Engineering effort since the hosted launch has gone mostly to proof records and to aerial and worker custody, not to planner jobs:

- From 2026-10-10 to `d4ca416`, **83 percent of all added lines (800,327 of 961,386) went to `docs/reviews/`.**
- In the same period, product source outside tests grew by 31,953 lines and tests by 57,927 lines.
- 402 dated review folders were created after October 7.

Meanwhile, basic nationwide paths are broken in ways a planner outside California would hit on the first day:

- Saving crash data for any Nevada study area queries California's crash source and stores nothing.
- Every county model run shows a California CEQA determination panel, whatever the state.
- A tribal reservation cannot be a workspace place.
- An MPO's uploaded network is never used by a model run.

The most valuable next work is to prove one real job end to end across several states, including a tribe, and to cut the per-change proof overhead until product change again outweighs paperwork.

### 4.3 Migration installation procedure

**What AGENTS.md requires.** Main is production. Every schema change requires these steps, in order (`AGENTS.md:185-194`):
1. Record the plan in the coordination file.
2. Take a fresh capture of the database and Storage.
3. Apply the migration by hand.
4. Confirm it.
5. Merge the dependent code.
6. Check the route that uses it.

**The cost.** At 144 migrations a month, that is about five manual production schema changes a day, each with a capture. There is no staging Supabase project. Merge slots are one PR at a time with clean main checks between merges, so a slot costs 50 to 130 minutes of wall time. That caps sequential throughput at roughly 10 to 25 merges a day, before any review.

**What it buys and what it misses.** The procedure is careful and has prevented data loss. But the volume of migrations is itself a choice. Many October migrations correct or extend migrations from the previous week, such as 424, 426 and the "parser correction" chain.

### 4.4 Proof regime

The rules in `AGENTS.md:104-119` and `MILESTONES.md` require the following for each change:
- a surviving harmless mutation and a targeted failing mutation for every changed test or guard;
- for every visible change, an identified-build browser journey at desktop and 390px with console review;
- a dated record retained in the repository.

Each rule is sound alone. Together, applied to every bounded change, they produce what section 4.1 measured: about 25 lines of evidence for every line of product source.

**The proof does not reach the place-specific paths.** The Nevada crash defect and the California CEQA leak passed all of it, because the proof runs on California and Oregon fixtures. The custody guards are excellent. The geographic gaps are wide.

### 4.5 Coordination overhead

**Sources of overhead:**
- Agents coordinate through a 1,606-line file outside the repository.
- They must read about 541 KB of governing documents before choosing a lane.
- `MILESTONES.md` (171 KB) is described as a verbatim move with five permitted kinds of edit plus an amendment table of 16 rows.
- The roadmap's prose header is longer than its status table. It holds seven dated paragraphs, each noting that it "changes no milestone status or direction-review binding."

**What it costs:**
- A fresh agent spends a large share of its context on process before reading code.
- The documents contradict each other on simple facts. `AGENTS.md:188` says "The dated catch-up records 405 migrations." Production has 448. `ARCHITECTURE.md:5` says it "has not been re-reconciled since September 6."

### 4.6 Verdict on process

The process is now slowing delivery toward v1. It protects money, custody and RLS well, and that protection should stay. But it has three faults:
- It charges the same proof cost to a label fix as to a money trigger.
- It runs the slowest worker suite on every change.
- It produces records faster than anyone reads them. The expired registries, the 0/0 packet inventory and the stale migration count are the evidence.

## 5. Required perspectives

### 5.1 Transportation and travel-model science

The scientific discipline in the code is better than in most commercial tools:
- The claim tier only downgrades.
- The engines are never averaged.
- Unknown is not zero.
- The 43.3 percent figure is correctly retired as a one-county selection metric.

The structure is still a prototype:
- ActivitySim runs Bay Area coefficients.
- Count ingestion is off by default outside two bundled California counties.
- No untouched holdout has been evaluated anywhere.
- Nine science drafts have been parked since October 23.

The contract's requirement is that both engines pass untouched validation in every state. The repository has no state with that result, and no frozen successor preregistration that would let one be produced (`NATIONWIDE_VALIDATION_PREREGISTRATION_V1.json` blocks new candidates until a successor freezes datasets and partitions). The modeler's interoperability gap matters as much as accuracy. No MPO will adopt a model it cannot load its own network into or export from as a layer.

### 5.2 Land use, statutory and development planning

Land Use Plans has a real authoring, review, adoption and publication flow, and plan-owned authority work has progressed (M1, PR 330 and later). What is missing:
- Housing element and RHNA work (grep 0 in `src`).
- Development review cases (5 incidental hits).
- Traffic impact studies outside California's VMT framing. The trip-generation defaults come from the San Diego 2003 manual, labeled as California screening rates (`lib/models/ite-rates.ts:1-17`).

For a city planner, development review is the daily job, and OpenPlan has no home for it. The CEQA panel showing on non-California county runs is a statutory leak in the opposite direction.

### 5.3 Environmental, climate, resilience and equity

Essentially templates:
- No hazard data adapter. FEMA, flood, wildfire and sea-level terms appear only in templates and text, in 14 files.
- No NEPA workflow beyond stage-gate checklist lines.
- No climate or GHG inventory beyond model KPIs.

Equity has CEJST bundled and a transit service-equity calculation with no default thresholds, which is well designed. The contract's environmental cell is "not-assessed." From the code, I would grade it "missing."

### 5.4 Community engagement, Title VI and public decisions

This is the strongest module. The gaps are the ones in section 3.5:
- demonstration text in templates;
- no CAPTCHA;
- Mapbox-only address search;
- no individual staff reply;
- no LEP four-factor or participation Title VI report;
- no populated-campaign export proof.

The "better than Social Pinpoint" requirement cannot be met by agent-run checks alone; see section 7.

### 5.5 Capital programming, grants, delivery and reimbursement

There is real depth in OWP and in contract accounting. The money-integrity fixes in PR 252 show that the triggers are taken seriously. But:
- There is no CIP object and no federal TIP or STIP type outside California's program lanes.
- No LAPM forms are generated.
- Obligations are dates without amounts.
- Grant discovery has no dates.
- Local-measure recipients cannot log in.

The pre-award to post-award chain exists as records. It does not exist as a package that a funder accepts.

### 5.6 Rural, tribal, small-agency and capacity-constrained use

This is where the product is weakest relative to its contract:
- The tribal gaps are structural: geography, funding and data control.
- A rural Nevada county gets no saved crash data.
- A small agency needs an operator to set three keys and run up to seven worker types to get maps, exports and models.
- The hosted site helps here, but only Nathaniel's demo workspace has an AI key, and the hosted site lacks county runs and aerial.

On a small agency's one-person staff, the first-run checklist leads with an Anthropic key (`module-map.md`, October 12; not re-verified).

### 5.7 GIS, data interoperability, evidence custody and public records

**Strengths:**
- Custody is the product's best engineering: exact hashes, retained originals, and owner, other-workspace and anonymous access checks.
- A project GeoPackage export exists.

**Gaps:**
- Model output has no GeoPackage export.
- Uploaded networks are unused.
- There is no GMNS support.
- There is no records-request, retention or legal-hold code (grep for `records.request|legal.hold|retention_polic` returns 0).
- A real agency answering a public records act request has no tool.

### 5.8 Agency operations, collaboration, accessibility, installation and recovery

**Strengths:**
- The restore drill passes in CI (26 of 26).
- A daily offsite encrypted capture exists.

**Gaps:**
- Production deep health and drift checks are off.
- There is no CAPTCHA.
- M3's "clean install" and "independent-host recovery" are open.
- Roles are owner, admin, member and viewer only, so there is no case-level confidentiality.
- No automated accessibility tool exists.
- The hosted Supabase is on Micro compute with a 1 GiB Storage limit, and the Railway hard cap is $40. These are fine for trials and not a plan for agency data volumes.

### 5.9 Adversarial product strategy: what has every prior agent missed?

Three things. None is subtle, and all are visible from code.

1. **No test runs the product in a state it was not built in.** Every journey fixture is California, Oregon's Deschutes County or Puerto Rico. The Nevada crash defect is a one-line resolver choice that a 51-state table would have caught. The contract's central promise, every state, has no executable check.
2. **The evidence system has become the main output.** Zero proven cells after three months of proof work means the proof is not aimed at closing cells. Proof work goes to custody of byte streams for a worker nobody can run on the hosted site.
3. **The first-week journeys have not been rerun in eight weeks, 28 versions.** That rerun is the cheapest available measure of whether the product works, and it is item 2 of the active queue.

## 6. The ten protocol questions

### Q1. If you were a planner contributing this software to the profession for free, what would make it the ultimate planning operating system?

Five properties, in order of how much a working planner would notice them:

1. **It knows my place on day one.** I name my county, city, tribe, MPO or state. It loads the free public data that applies:
   - crashes, in the richest source for my state;
   - Census and LODES;
   - OSM network, GTFS and counts;
   - my state's grant programs with dates;
   - the statutes that apply to my plan type.

   It shows plainly what it could not find. Today this works in California and partly in Oregon.
2. **It produces the documents I am judged on, in the form my reviewer accepts.** That means a staff report, a plan chapter, a grant application with its attachments, a funder's claim form and a board packet. Records are necessary, but planners are evaluated on documents.
3. **It interoperates with what my agency already has.** Shapefile, GeoPackage and GMNS in and out. Our own network in the model. Exports that open in ArcGIS and QGIS without cleanup.
4. **It joins the work.** One project carries geography, evidence, money, comments and decisions from scoping through closeout. The architecture aims at this; the CIP and TIP gap and the module islands still break it.
5. **Its numbers are honest and labeled.** This is the existing strength: claim tiers, never-averaged engines and unknown kept distinct from zero. Keep it.

OpenPlan has the hardest part of property 5 right and much of 4 designed. It is weakest on 1 outside California and on 2 everywhere.

### Q2. Can every type of US planner do their core work here? Name the missing or shallow jobs.

**No.** These jobs are missing or shallow, from code:

- **Tribal transportation planner:** no AIANNH geography, no TTP, TTIP or TTPSF, no data-sovereignty controls (missing).
- **State DOT planner:** no state place kind; statewide plan, STIP, freight plan and performance-target jobs are absent. PM1, PM2 and PM3 target setting appears in 4 files (shallow to missing).
- **MPO modeler:** no own-network import, no GMNS, no model-output GeoPackage, borrowed coefficients (shallow).
- **MPO programmer:** no federal TIP type, no amendment and administrative-modification workflow, no fiscal-constraint-by-year TIP (missing outside the RTP's fiscal element).
- **City current planner:** no development review case, no conditions of approval, no housing element or sites inventory (missing).
- **Public works and capital:** no CIP object, no agency-defined scoring, no LAPM forms, obligation without amounts, no pavement or asset management (5 incidental hits) (shallow).
- **ADA coordinator:** no ADA transition plan (grep 0). This is outside the current module map and is a core local-agency obligation (missing).
- **Title VI coordinator:** service equity only; no LEP four-factor and no Title VI program document (shallow).
- **Records officer:** no records request, retention or hold (missing).
- **Transit service planner:** GTFS intake and equity exist; no service-change plan or service-standard monitoring (grep 0) (shallow).
- **Environmental planner:** templates only (missing).
- **Grant writer:** organized but not submittable; no dates and no attachments (shallow).
- **Engagement lead:** usable, with the gaps in section 3.5.
- **OWP administrator:** usable, the deepest path.

Outside the current module map: ADA transition plans, records requests, congestion management process (grep 0), staff reports and agendas (8 incidental hits), and federal performance-target reporting.

### Q3. Does every state work in substance, not merely degrade honestly?

**No, and some states do not even degrade honestly.**
- Forty-eight states plus DC are unregistered for readiness.
- Six states have grant bundles.
- California alone has a state crash source.
- Four states have count adapters: CA, WA, CO and OR.

Honest degradation fails in two verified places:
- Nevada crash saves query California and store nothing, and the result is labeled with CCRS's richer coverage (the label is inferred).
- Every county run outside California shows a California CEQA screening determination.

### Q4. Is California the gold-standard implementation across all its geographies and agency types?

**California is the deepest implementation, but not gold standard.**
- The LAPM profile hard-codes District 3.
- LAPM forms are deferred.
- Housing element work is absent.
- Model validation counts cover two Sierra foothill counties, not coastal, metropolitan, border or tribal California.
- The California tribal community-plan definition is not selectable.

The contract's "flawless" definition requires every California county and place to resolve from registry data. Place resolution appears to do that. The listed agency types (RTPA, MPO, county, city, tribe) do not all have working paths.

### Q5. Are both travel models scientifically validated for every published use, in every state, without a national aggregate hiding regional failure?

**No.** Neither model is validated for any use in any state against an untouched holdout. The product says so on its surfaces; the claim tiers are honest. The aggregate-hiding risk is controlled by policy. What is missing is evidence, not policy. The blocking item is a frozen successor preregistration, and nine drafts toward it sit unmerged.

### Q6. What is the simplest overlooked idea with the largest product effect?

**A 51-row state table that every core journey runs against.** Pick one representative county per state and DC, plus a tribal area and a multistate MPO. Record the expected answer for each source decision:
- crash source for ingest and for read;
- grant bundle or "none registered";
- count source;
- statute panels allowed;
- readiness entry.

Run it in CI with recorded fixtures. Most of it is a data file and a parameterized test. It would have caught the Nevada crash defect, the CEQA leak and the missing readiness entries. It turns "all 50 states" from a contract sentence into a number that moves each week.

### Q7. Which old rule, agent decision, architecture or roadmap item is now wrong?

1. **"Every changed test or guard needs a harmless surviving mutation and a targeted failing one"** (`AGENTS.md:104-105`), applied universally. Keep it for money, RLS, custody and scientific-claim guards. For the rest, a normal failing-first test is enough. Evidence: tests are now 0.91 of source lines and the guards missed the place-specific defects.
2. **A dated repository review folder for each bounded change.** 467 folders, 193 MB and 83 percent of recent additions. Move per-change evidence into PR descriptions and CI artifacts. Keep dated folders for direction reviews, releases, science and incidents.
3. **Running the aerial custody regression on every PR.** Path-filter it to `workers/odm_worker/**` and its callers.
4. **Main-is-production with hand-applied migrations at five a day and no staging database.** Add a staging Supabase project, or at minimum batch migrations per release.
5. **The aerial lane's place in sequencing.** It took the largest single share of engineering after October 10. It cannot run on the hosted site. Its main user is a planner with a drone and a local GPU, which is a narrow subset. The contract keeps full aerial work, and I do not propose removing it. I propose it wait behind the nationwide basics.
6. **"Maps" as a module that cannot make a map without the planner's paid Claude subscription.** This contradicts "no required paid service" for a core artifact. Either OpenPlan renders map packages itself, through the existing MapLibre path and a template, or Maps leaves the top level until it can.
7. **The October 17 policy that agent-run checks fully replace human feedback before v1.** See section 7.
8. **The capability matrix as the v1 instrument, in its current form.** It has 99 abstract dimension cells, none proven, and it is four weeks past review. A dimension like "tribal governments" cannot be proven alone. The matrix should be re-cut into named jobs bound to place, such as "a rural Nevada county completes an SS4A safety action plan." Each such job closes several dimension cells at once.
9. **`AGENTS.md:188` "405 migrations"** and `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s September 6 baseline. These are stale facts in the canonical entry documents; they need correction.

### Q8. What should be removed, joined or made deeper before anything new is added?

**Remove or demote:**
- The `/billing` and `/command-center` redirects; give them removal dates.
- Generic Plans, which overlaps RTP and Safety and has no writing tools; join it into Projects or the statutory plans.
- Maps from the top level until it renders in-app.
- Model Validation (County Runs) as a top-level item; fold it under Models.
- Demonstration text in public-facing template fields.

**Join:**

- The capital pieces (awards, milestones, phases, invoices, stage gates) under a project-phase budget.
- Engagement synthesis's 28 tables into fewer. That is an engineering simplification; I did not read every table and would want the owner to confirm.

**Make deeper:**
- Place handling: tribal, state and MPO kinds, and per-state crash, count and grant sources.
- Model interoperability: own network in, GeoPackage out.
- Documents: a staff report and a funder-accepted packet.
