# Where the two demand models agree

**AequilibraE gravity (trip-based)** compared against **ActivitySim prototype_mtc (activity-based)**, assigned on the same network with the same assignment settings.

Of 3,520 links carrying more than 100 vehicles, 276 agree closely between the two demand models and 3,133 diverge. Agreement is judged by GEH, the traffic-engineering yardstick for comparing two volumes — but its usual thresholds were written for checking a model against measured counts, where one side is ground truth. Here neither side is, so agreement means the two methods concur, NOT that either is correct. The two are never averaged: a blended figure would have no provenance and would destroy the only thing this comparison produces.

Both runs loaded the same 28,670 links, so the network was genuinely held constant and a difference in a link's volume is attributable to the demand model.

Both runs converged tightly enough that the assignment is not itself generating divergence, so a difference here is attributable to the demand model link by link as well as corridor by corridor.

## What this is not

- Neither method is ground truth. Agreement means the two concur; it does not mean either is correct, and both can be wrong in the same direction.
- The two volumes are never averaged. A blended figure has no defensible provenance and destroys the only signal this comparison produces.
- GEH thresholds are borrowed from model-versus-count validation, where one side is measured. They are used here because they are the yardstick a reviewer recognises, not because a validation standard has been met.
- The assignment's own contribution was measured for this network by re-assigning one model's own demand and comparing it against itself: 0.0% of busy links diverged with no demand difference at all, at a relative gap of 0.00044719891533249675. Divergence at or below that level is the assignment deciding between parallel routes, not the demand models disagreeing.
- Both runs converged tightly enough that the assignment is not itself generating divergence, so a difference here is attributable to the demand model link by link as well as corridor by corridor.
