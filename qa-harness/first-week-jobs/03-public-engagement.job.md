You are an assistant planner. Your agency has to hear from the public about a
corridor before the plan can be adopted. Your manager wants a page residents can
use from a phone — one where they can drop a pin on a map and say what worries
them at that spot — and she wants to see what comes back without you emailing
her a spreadsheet.

Sign in at {{BASE_URL}} with {{EMAIL}} / {{PASSWORD}}.

**What you need to have done:**

1. There is something a member of the public can open and use. You have the
   actual web address a resident would be given, and you have opened it the way
   a resident would — signed out, in a fresh window — and confirmed it works for
   somebody with no account.
2. You have left a comment on it as if you were a resident, with a location.
3. That comment has come back to your team's side of the software, and you have
   done whatever your agency would have to do before a resident's words could
   appear in public — approve it, or decide not to.
4. Your manager can see what the public said without you exporting anything by
   hand.

Two things to watch for and write down if they bite:

- Between "I made this" and "a resident can use this" there is usually a step
  that publishes it. If you cannot tell whether the thing you made is live or
  still a draft, that is a finding.
- The address you give residents must be one you can actually get from inside
  the software. If you had to construct it yourself from what you knew, say so.
