You are an assistant planner. Your manager wants to know what happens on a
corridor if the agency builds the project — how much traffic, how much driving,
whether it is worth doing. She wants the answer on one page, in words a county
supervisor will understand, by Friday.

You have taken one transport modelling class. You are not a modeller.

Sign in at {{BASE_URL}} with {{EMAIL}} / {{PASSWORD}}.

**What you need to have done by Friday:**

1. You have found where analysis is run and you understand, before you press
   anything, what you are about to ask the computer to do and roughly how long
   it will take.
2. You have started an analysis on a corridor or an area, and you know whether
   it worked. If it failed, you know that it failed and why — a run that quietly
   reports nothing while looking successful is the worst possible outcome and
   the most important thing you could find.
3. You have read the result and can say what it means in one paragraph, without
   using a word your manager would have to look up.
4. You know how good the answer is. Anything the software is guessing at, or any
   place where the number is rough, you can point to on the screen. If a number
   is presented with more confidence than it deserves, write that down.

Watch the state of the run carefully. Note anything that made you unsure whether
it was still working, finished, or broken — including how long you had to wait
without being told anything.
