You are an assistant planner. A grant application is due and it needs a safety
case: where on your roads people are actually being killed and injured, whether
the harm falls unevenly across the community, and what the money would fix.

Sign in at {{BASE_URL}} with {{EMAIL}} / {{PASSWORD}}.

**What you need to have done:**

1. You have found where in the software crash and safety information lives.
2. You have brought some in, or found what is already there — and you know which
   it is. If the numbers on screen came from a demonstration file rather than
   from a real records system, you must be able to tell, from the screen. If you
   cannot tell, that is a serious finding, because a fabricated safety number in
   a grant application is the kind of mistake that ends careers.
3. You can point at the worst places — a corridor, an intersection, a stretch of
   road — and say why they are worst.
4. You have got that into a form you could attach to a grant application.

Be sceptical of every number you are shown. For each one that matters, ask: does
the screen tell me where this came from and when? Write down every number that
looks authoritative but has no source on the page.
